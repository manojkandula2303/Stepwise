=== p-workplace-behaviour | Workplace Behaviour
summary: Professionalism, punctuality, respect, confidentiality and ownership in everyday situations.
---
These questions check whether you behave like a **dependable professional**.

## Principles

- **Reliability:** do what you said, on time; warn people early if you can't.
- **Respect:** treat everyone with courtesy, whatever their level.
- **Confidentiality:** keep company and client information private.
- **Ownership:** admit mistakes quickly and fix them.
- **Process:** follow the agreed policies, and raise concerns through the proper channels.

- [x] Inform your manager or HR as soon as possible, with an expected arrival time
- Take the day off
- Blame the train company when you arrive
> Communicate early and give an estimate.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A friend from another company asks about your client's upcoming product. What do you do?
- Share the general details
- [x] Politely decline; client information is confidential
- Share it if they promise secrecy
- Share only the launch date
> Confidentiality isn't optional.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: You accidentally delete a shared file. What is the best response?
- Hope nobody notices
- [x] Tell the team immediately and work on restoring it from a backup
- Blame a system glitch
- Recreate it silently and say nothing
> Owning it quickly limits the damage.
:::

- [x] Speak to them privately about the impact; escalate if it continues
- Post about it online
- Ignore it forever
> Address it directly and respectfully first.

- Offer help, and ask for help early.
- Address problems with teammates **directly and privately** before escalating.

=== p-leadership | Leadership
summary: Taking initiative, delegating, motivating others and making decisions when leading a team or task.
---
Leadership questions for freshers focus on **initiative and influence**, not titles: stepping up, guiding peers and taking responsibility for outcomes.

## Principles

- Set a clear goal and roles.
- Delegate based on people's strengths, and support them rather than micromanaging.
- Listen before deciding; then decide and explain why.
- Take responsibility for the team's failures, and share its successes.

=== p-decision-making | Decision Making
summary: Gathering information, weighing options, deciding under time pressure and owning the outcome.
---
Good decisions are **informed, timely and accountable**.

## A practical process

1. Clarify the goal and the constraints (time, budget, rules).
2. Gather the key facts; don't wait for perfect information.
3. Compare options against the criteria.
4. Decide, communicate and act.
5. Review the outcome and learn.

:::mcq medium
Q: You must choose between two vendors by the end of the day, and the data is incomplete. What should you do?
- Delay until all the data arrives
- [x] Decide with the best available information, document your assumptions and inform stakeholders
- Choose the cheaper one without thinking
- Ask someone else to decide
> A timely decision with transparent assumptions.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Before making a major decision that affects the team, what is the most effective step?
- Decide alone, quickly
- [x] Consult the people affected and the relevant experts
- Announce the decision without explanation
- Postpone it
> Input improves both the decision and buy-in.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Your decision turns out to be wrong. What should you do?
- Hide it
- [x] Acknowledge it, correct course quickly and share what you learned
- Blame the data
- Defend it regardless
> Accountability plus learning.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Which is the best way to compare options?
- Go with your gut only
- [x] Score them against agreed criteria such as cost, time, risk and impact
- Choose whatever the senior person prefers
- Choose randomly
> Structured comparison reduces bias.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A customer asks for something outside your authority to approve. What should you do?
- Approve it anyway
- [x] Explain that you'll check with the right person and get back to them quickly
- Refuse without explanation
- Ignore the request
> Respect the limits of your authority while staying helpful.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Facing a decision under extreme time pressure, what should you prioritise?
- Perfection
- [x] The most important factors, especially safety, customer impact and policy
- What's easiest for you
- Avoiding any decision
> Focus on what matters most.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Two options seem equally good. What is a sensible tie-breaker?
- Choose the riskier one
- [x] Choose the option that is easier to reverse if it doesn't work
- Choose neither
- Ask for a vote of strangers
> Reversibility reduces risk.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: After a decision is made, what is the most important next step?
- Move on and never revisit it
- [x] Communicate it clearly, assign actions and review the results later
- Keep debating it
- Keep it secret
> Execution and review complete the process.
:::

=== p-problem-solving | Problem Solving
summary: Defining the problem, finding root causes, generating options and testing solutions.
---
Employers value people who **diagnose before they fix** and who are creative but practical.

## Approach

1. **Define** the problem precisely: what, where, when, and its impact.
2. **Find the root cause.** Ask "why" repeatedly (the 5 Whys); don't just treat the symptoms.
3. **Generate options**, including simple ones.
4. **Choose and test** on a small scale where possible.
5. **Prevent recurrence:** fix the process, not just the instance.

:::mcq medium
Q: Customers report that an app crashes "sometimes". What is the most effective first step?
- Rewrite the whole app
- [x] Gather details such as the device, steps and time to reproduce and narrow down the problem
- Tell customers to reinstall it
- Ignore it until it happens more often
> Define the problem precisely first.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: What is the "5 Whys" technique for?
- Delaying decisions
- [x] Finding the root cause by repeatedly asking why
- Assigning blame
- Brainstorming product names
> It digs past the symptoms.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A recurring billing error is fixed manually each month. What is the best long-term action?
- Keep fixing it manually
- [x] Find and fix the root cause in the process or system
- Hire someone to do the manual fix
- Stop billing customers
> Prevent recurrence.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: You're stuck on a problem after trying everything you can think of. What should you do?
- Give up
- [x] Explain it to a colleague or seek fresh input, sharing what you've already tried
- Pretend it's solved
- Wait for it to fix itself
> Fresh perspectives unlock problems.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: You have a risky solution and a safe one. How should you proceed?
- Roll out the risky one everywhere at once
- [x] Test it on a small scale first, then expand if it works
- Never try new solutions
- Pick the safe one without considering the benefits
> Small experiments limit the risk.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Sales dropped last month. Which is the best problem statement?
- "Sales are bad."
- [x] "Online sales in the South region fell 18% in May, mainly in repeat customers."
- "Something is wrong."
- "Marketing failed."
> It is specific and measurable.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: When brainstorming solutions, what is most helpful?
- Criticising every idea immediately
- [x] Generating many options first, then evaluating them
- Accepting only the first idea
- Letting only seniors speak
> Separate generating ideas from judging them.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: Your fix stops the error, but you don't understand why it works. What should you do next?
- Ship it and move on
- [x] Investigate until you understand the cause, so the fix doesn't hide a deeper issue
- Revert it
- Add more fixes
> Understanding prevents fragile solutions.
:::

=== p-conflict | Conflict Management
summary: Resolving disagreements with colleagues, managers and clients calmly and constructively.
---
Conflict isn't bad in itself. **Unmanaged** conflict is. Strong candidates handle it **directly, privately and respectfully**, focusing on interests rather than egos.

## Approach

1. Stay calm, and listen to understand the other person's view.
2. Talk privately, and focus on the issue, not the person.
3. Find common goals and explore the options.
4. Agree on next steps; escalate only if direct resolution fails.

:::mcq medium

- [x] Speak to them privately about it, and in future share ideas in writing as well
- Complain to everyone

> Address it calmly, and prevent it happening again.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: You and a teammate disagree strongly on a technical approach. What should you do?
- Insist on your way
- [x] Discuss the pros and cons against the project's goals, and test or ask for input if you're still stuck
- Let them win to avoid conflict
- Stop talking to them
> Use objective criteria.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Your manager criticises your work in front of others. What is the best response?
- Argue back immediately
- [x] Stay composed, then discuss the feedback and how it was delivered privately later
- Quit
- Cry and leave the room
> Composure, followed by a private conversation.
:::

:::mcq medium

- Fire one of them
- Take sides with the better performer

:::

:::mcq medium

- End the contract
- Ignore their emails

:::

:::mcq medium

- Walking out
- Making a joke at their expense

:::

:::mcq medium
Q: When should you escalate a conflict to your manager?

- [x] After genuine attempts to resolve it directly have failed, or when it involves policy or ethics breaches
- Never
- Only through anonymous complaints

=== p-time-management | Time Management
summary: Prioritising with urgency and importance, managing deadlines and saying no constructively.
---
## Tools

- **Eisenhower matrix:** do **urgent and important** tasks now, schedule **important but not urgent** ones, delegate **urgent but not important** ones, and drop the rest.
- **Break big tasks** into small, scheduled steps.
- **Time-box** your work, and protect focus time.
- **Communicate early** when deadlines are at risk.

:::mcq medium
Q: You have three tasks: a client deliverable due today, a training module due next week, and routine emails. What should you do first?
- The emails
- [x] The client deliverable due today
- The training module
- Whichever is easiest
> It is urgent and important.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: In the Eisenhower matrix, what should you do with "important but not urgent" tasks?
- Ignore them
- [x] Schedule time for them
- Do them immediately
- Delegate them all
> Planning time for these prevents crises later.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Your manager adds a new urgent task while you're already at full capacity. What should you do?
- Silently try to do everything
- [x] Ask which existing task can be deprioritised or delayed
- Refuse the new task

> Make the trade-offs visible.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A large assignment is due in three weeks. What is the best approach?

- [x] Break it into milestones and schedule each one
- Wait for inspiration
- Do it only on weekends
> Milestones prevent last-minute rushes.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: You keep getting interrupted while doing focused work. What helps most?

- [x] Blocking focus time and batching messages at set times
- Answering every message instantly
- Multitasking more
> Protect your deep-work time.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: You realise you can't finish a task by the deadline. What should you do?

- [x] Inform the stakeholder early, with progress, a new estimate and options
- Miss the deadline without notice
- Ask a colleague to finish it without telling anyone

:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Which habit wastes the most time?
- Planning the day
- [x] Constant task-switching
- Taking short breaks
- Prioritising

:::

:::mcq hard
Q: A colleague often asks you for help, which delays your own deadlines. What is the best approach?

- Notice your own triggers, and pause before reacting.
- Read others' emotions from their tone and body language.
- Respond to feelings first, then to facts.
- Receive feedback without being defensive.

- Tell others about it
- Ask them to cheer up
> Empathy with respect for their privacy.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Which component of EI means managing your own emotional reactions?

:::mcq medium

=== p-stress | Stress Management

Employers want people who stay **composed and effective under pressure**, and who recognise when to ask for help.

## Strategies

- Break overwhelming work into small next actions.
- Prioritise ruthlessly, and communicate your capacity.
- Use short breaks, breathing exercises and good sleep; they genuinely improve performance.
- Ask for help or resources before burnout, not after.

- Take the day off
> Structure reduces stress.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Which is a healthy way to manage work stress?

- [x] Regular breaks, exercise and adequate sleep
- Bottling up your feelings
- Working through every weekend
> Recovery sustains your performance.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: You're struggling with a workload that is consistently unmanageable. What should you do?

- [x] Discuss your workload and priorities honestly with your manager

- Do lower-quality work

:::

=== p-ethics | Workplace Ethics
summary: Honesty, fairness, conflicts of interest, data privacy and reporting misconduct.
---
Ethics questions have a clear pattern: **choose honesty, fairness and the policy-compliant action**, even when it is inconvenient.

## Key areas

- **Honesty:** never falsify data, reports or attendance.
- **Conflict of interest:** declare personal interests that could affect your decisions.
- **Privacy:** protect customer and employee data.
- **Fairness:** no favouritism or discrimination.
- **Reporting:** raise misconduct through the proper channels (your manager, HR, an ethics hotline).

:::mcq medium

=== p-integrity | Integrity
summary: Consistency between words and actions: owning mistakes, keeping commitments and being honest in assessments.
---
**Integrity** means doing the right thing when no one is watching, and keeping your word. Integrity questions often test small, tempting shortcuts.

## Markers of integrity

- Admitting mistakes rather than hiding them
- Giving credit where it is due
- Being honest about your skills and progress
- Keeping your commitments, or renegotiating them early
- Answering assessments truthfully
