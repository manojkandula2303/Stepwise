=== p-personality | Personality Assessments
summary: What personality tests measure (the Big Five), how employers use them, and how to answer honestly and consistently. Includes a self-assessment.
---
Many companies (Accenture, Capgemini, Deloitte, banks) include a **personality or behavioural questionnaire**. There are **no right answers**. Employers look for **fit** with the role and for **consistent, honest** responses.

## The Big Five (OCEAN)

| Trait | High scorers tend to be… | Low scorers tend to be… |
|---|---|---|
| **O**penness | curious, imaginative, open to new ideas | practical, conventional, prefer the familiar |
| **C**onscientiousness | organised, dependable, goal-driven | flexible, spontaneous, less structured |
| **E**xtraversion | outgoing, energised by people | reserved, energised by solitude |
| **A**greeableness | cooperative, trusting, empathetic | direct, sceptical, competitive |
| **N**euroticism (emotional reactivity) | prone to stress and worry | calm, emotionally steady |

## How to approach these tests

- **Answer honestly.** Tests include repeated and reversed items to detect "faking good". Inconsistent answers are a red flag.
- **Think about your behaviour at work or college**, not your ideal self.
- **Avoid extremes for everything.** Marking "strongly agree" on every positive statement looks unrealistic.
- **Don't overthink.** Your first instinct is usually the most accurate.

=== p-work-preferences | Work Preferences
summary: Work styles such as structure vs flexibility, team vs solo, detail vs big picture and pace, and how they match roles. Includes a self-assessment.
---
Work-preference questionnaires ask **how you like to work**. They help match you to roles and teams, and help you answer interview questions such as "Describe your ideal work environment."

## Common dimensions

| Dimension | One end | Other end |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | clear processes and plans | flexibility and improvisation |
| Collaboration | team-based work | independent work |
| Focus | detail and precision | the big picture and strategy |
| Pace | steady and predictable | fast-paced and varied |
| Motivation | mastery and learning | recognition and impact |

## Matching preferences to roles (examples)

- **Detail + structure:** QA, finance, compliance, backend engineering
- **Big picture + flexibility:** product management, consulting, start-ups
- **Collaboration + fast pace:** client services, sales engineering, agile delivery teams
- **Independent + deep focus:** research, data science, systems programming

## In interviews

Be honest, and show **adaptability**: *"I work best with clear goals, but I'm comfortable adjusting when priorities change."*

=== p-sjt | Situational Judgement Tests
summary: How SJTs work, what employers measure, and a method for picking the most effective response.
---
A **Situational Judgement Test (SJT)** describes a realistic work scenario and asks which response is **most effective** (sometimes also the least effective, or a ranking).

## What employers measure

Responsibility · integrity · teamwork · customer focus · problem-solving · composure · respect for process and people.

## How to choose the best response

1. **Identify the core issue:** a deadline? a colleague? a customer? an ethical breach?
2. **Prefer responses that:**
   - take **ownership** and act **promptly**
   - **solve the problem** while respecting people
   - **communicate** with the right person (often your manager, in the end)
   - follow **policy and ethics**
3. **Avoid responses that:**
   - ignore or delay the problem
   - blame others or escalate too quickly, without trying first
   - break rules, hide mistakes or overstep your authority
   - are aggressive or passive

:::tip
The best answer usually balances **action** (you do something useful now) with **appropriate communication** (you keep the right people informed).
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: You notice a small error in a report your team has already sent to a client. What is the most effective response?
- Ignore it; it's small
- Wait to see whether the client notices
- [x] Inform your manager, prepare a correction and suggest sending it promptly
- Blame the colleague who prepared it
> Take ownership, communicate and fix it quickly.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Your manager gives you a task with an unclear goal. What should you do?
- Guess and start immediately
- [x] Ask clarifying questions about the goal, scope and deadline before starting
- Wait until the deadline and ask then

> Clarifying first prevents wasted effort.
:::

:::mcq medium

- Hang up

:::

:::mcq medium

:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A teammate is struggling with a task that you know how to do, and your own work is on track. What should you do?

- [x] Offer to help or show them how, without neglecting your own work

:::

Q: In SJTs, which kind of response is usually the least effective?

:::
