=== v-reading-comprehension | Reading Comprehension
summary: Main idea, detail, inference, vocabulary-in-context and tone questions on short passages.
---
Reading comprehension (RC) has five question types. Each needs a slightly different kind of reading.

| Type | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Main idea** | Choose the option that covers the **whole** passage, not one detail. |
| **Detail** | Find the line in the passage that answers it; don't rely on memory. |
| **Inference** | Choose what **must** be true from the passage, the smallest logical step. |
| **Vocabulary in context** | Replace the word with each option and reread the sentence. |
| **Tone / attitude** | Look for evaluative words (unfortunately, rightly, merely). |

## Approach

1. Skim the passage once for structure: what is each paragraph doing?
2. Read the question, then go back to the relevant lines.
3. Eliminate options that are **too broad, too narrow, extreme, or not in the passage**.

:::warning
An option can be true in real life and still be wrong if the passage doesn't support it.
:::

:::context
**Passage 1**

Urban trees do far more than decorate streets. A mature tree can cool the air around it by several degrees through shade and evaporation, reducing the need for air conditioning in nearby buildings. Trees also absorb rainwater, easing pressure on drains during storms, and their leaves trap fine dust that would otherwise be inhaled. Despite these benefits, cities often treat trees as obstacles, cutting them down to widen roads or lay cables. Planners increasingly argue that trees should be counted as infrastructure, like pipes and power lines, and budgeted for accordingly. Doing so would mean valuing the services trees provide, not merely the cost of planting and pruning them.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: What is the main idea of the passage?
- Trees make streets look beautiful.
- [x] Urban trees provide valuable services and should be treated as infrastructure.
- Cities should stop building roads.
- Air conditioning is harmful to cities.
> It covers both the benefits and the planners' proposal.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: According to the passage, how do trees reduce pressure on drains?
- By cooling the air
- [x] By absorbing rainwater
- By trapping dust
- By shading buildings
> This is stated directly in the third sentence.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: As used in the passage, "accordingly" most nearly means:
- Reluctantly
- [x] Correspondingly
- Immediately
- Secretly
> It means budgeted in a way that matches treating trees as infrastructure.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: What is the author's attitude towards cutting down trees to widen roads?
- Enthusiastic
- Neutral
- [x] Critical
- Indifferent
> "Despite these benefits… treat trees as obstacles" signals disapproval.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Which of these is NOT mentioned as a benefit of urban trees?
- Cooling the air
- Trapping dust
- Absorbing rainwater
- [x] Attracting tourists
> Tourism isn't mentioned.
:::

:::context
**Passage 2**

For much of history, people seem to have slept in two shifts. Historical records from Europe describe a "first sleep" beginning soon after dusk, followed by an hour or so of wakefulness around midnight, and then a "second sleep" until morning. During the waking interval, people prayed, read, tended fires or visited neighbours. The pattern appears to have faded during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, as artificial lighting spread and evenings grew longer. Some sleep researchers now suggest that waking briefly at night is not necessarily a disorder, but may echo this older rhythm. They caution, however, that modern work schedules make a split night impractical for most people.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: What is the passage mainly about?
- How to cure insomnia
- [x] A historical two-part sleep pattern and its decline
- The invention of artificial lighting
- Night-time activities in Europe
> The other options are details or not covered.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: According to the passage, what caused the two-shift pattern to fade?
- Modern work schedules
- [x] The spread of artificial lighting
- Advice from doctors
- Religious changes
> "As artificial lighting spread and evenings grew longer."
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: What do some researchers suggest about waking briefly at night?
- It is a serious disorder.
- [x] It may not be a disorder.
- Everyone should do it.
- It improves productivity.
> "Not necessarily a disorder."
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: Which of these can be inferred from the passage?
- People in the past slept less overall.
- [x] Before artificial lighting, people tended to go to sleep soon after dark.
- Split sleep is common today.
- Researchers recommend split sleep.
> First sleep began "soon after dusk". The other options aren't supported.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: As used in the passage, "echo" most nearly means:
- Repeat loudly
- [x] Resemble
- Reject
- Replace
> Night waking may resemble or reflect the older rhythm.
:::

=== v-critical-reasoning | Critical Reasoning
summary: Assumptions, strengthening and weakening, inference, flaws and paradoxes in short arguments.
---
Every argument has a **conclusion**, **evidence** and an unstated **assumption** linking them.

## Question types

| Asks you to… | Look for |
|---|---|
| **Find the assumption** | the gap between evidence and conclusion; use the negation test |
| **Strengthen** | a fact that supports the assumption or rules out other explanations |
| **Weaken** | an alternative cause, a missing comparison, or a broken assumption |
| **Draw a conclusion / inference** | something that **must** follow from the facts given |
| **Identify the flaw** | over-generalisation, confusing correlation with cause, circular reasoning |
| **Resolve a paradox** | a fact that makes two seemingly conflicting facts both true |

**Correlation vs causation** is the most common flaw. "Crime fell after more street lights were installed" doesn't prove the lights caused it; a **control comparison** (nearby areas without lights) would.

:::mcq medium
Q: "Sales of our new phone will rise because we have cut its price." Which assumption does this argument rely on?
- The phone has a good camera.
- [x] Demand for the phone responds to its price.
- Competitors will raise their prices.
- The phone is the cheapest on the market.
> If price didn't affect demand, the conclusion would fail.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "Students who eat breakfast perform better in exams." Which finding best supports the claim that breakfast improves performance?
- Many students skip breakfast.
- [x] In a controlled study, students randomly given breakfast scored higher than those who skipped it.
- Breakfast cereals are popular.
- Top students often study late.
> Random assignment rules out other explanations.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "Crime in City X fell after more street lights were installed, so street lights reduce crime." Which fact most weakens this?
- Street lights are expensive.
- [x] Crime fell by the same amount in nearby cities that installed no new lights.
- City X has a large police force.
- Some residents dislike bright lights.
> It points to another cause affecting the whole region.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: "All employees who completed the training were promoted. Priya was not promoted." What follows?
- Priya completed the training.
- [x] Priya did not complete the training.
- Priya will be promoted next year.
- The training was optional.
> This is the contrapositive.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "Ravi has never had an accident, so he must be the safest driver in the city." What is the main flaw?
- It uses circular reasoning.
- [x] It draws a sweeping conclusion from limited evidence.
- It attacks Ravi personally.
- It relies on an expert's opinion.
> A clean record alone doesn't make him the safest driver.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "Every member of the chess club is in the maths club. Some maths club members play football." If the chess club has members, which must be true?
- Some chess players play football.
- [x] Some maths club members are in the chess club.
- All football players are in the chess club.
- No chess player plays football.
> Chess members are a subset of the maths club. The football link isn't certain.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: "Hospital admissions in a town rose after a new hospital opened, although residents' health did not get worse." What best explains this?
- The new hospital is expensive.
- [x] The new hospital made it easier for residents to get treatment for minor problems.
- The old hospital closed down years ago.
- Doctors moved to other cities.
> Easier access, not worse health, explains the rise.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "Our new advertising campaign caused last month's 20% rise in sales." Which fact most weakens this?
- The campaign was expensive.
- [x] A major competitor closed all its stores last month.
- Customers liked the advert's music.
- Sales rose in our online store too.
> It offers an alternative explanation for the sales rise.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "Working from home increases productivity." Which finding most strengthens this claim?
- Employees like flexible schedules.
- [x] Firms that switched to remote work saw output per employee rise while hours stayed the same.
- Offices are costly to rent.
- Some managers dislike remote work.
> It shows more output with the same input.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: "To reduce traffic jams, the city should build more roads." Which assumption does this argument rely on?
- Roads are cheap to build.
- [x] New roads will not simply attract proportionally more cars.
- People prefer public transport.
- The city has unused land.
> If new roads draw more traffic (induced demand), the plan fails.
:::
