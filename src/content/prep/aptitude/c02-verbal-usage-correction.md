=== v-tenses | Tenses
summary: The 12 tenses and when to use each one, with signal words such as since/for, by the time and yesterday.
---
## The tense table (verb: work)

| | Simple | Continuous | Perfect | Perfect continuous |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Present** | work(s) | am/is/are working | has/have worked | has/have been working |
| **Past** | worked | was/were working | had worked | had been working |
| **Future** | will work | will be working | will have worked | will have been working |

## Signal words

- **Simple present:** habits and facts (every day, always). He **goes** to school every day.
- **Present continuous:** happening now (look!, now). Look! It **is raining**.
- **Present perfect:** past action linked to now, with no specific time (ever, never, just, yet). I **have never been** to Paris.
- **Present perfect continuous:** started in the past and still going, with **since or for**. She **has been working** here since 2019.
- **Simple past:** a finished time (yesterday, last year, ago). I **met** him yesterday.
- **Past continuous:** in progress when something else happened. She **was cooking** when the phone rang.
- **Past perfect:** the earlier of two past actions. When I arrived, they **had left**.
- **Future perfect:** complete before a future point (by next year, by the time). By next year I **will have graduated**.

:::warning
Never use the present perfect with a finished-time word. ✘ "I have seen him yesterday" → ✔ "I saw him yesterday."
:::

## Conditionals

- If + present, will + base form: If it rains tomorrow, we **will stay** at home.
- In time clauses about the future, use the present: By the time you **reach**, the film **will have started**.

=== v-subject-verb-agreement | Subject–Verb Agreement
summary: Singular and plural subjects, words in between, either/neither, collective nouns and "a number" vs "the number".
---
A singular subject takes a singular verb and a plural subject takes a plural verb. The traps are in **finding the real subject**.

## Rules

1. **Ignore words in between:** The list *of items* **is** on the desk.
2. **Each, every, either, neither, one of** are singular: Each of the boys **gets** a prize. One of my friends **is** a doctor.
3. **Either/neither … or/nor:** the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject. Neither John nor his friends **are** coming.
4. **"As well as", "along with", "with"** don't make a subject plural: He, as well as his friends, **is** coming.
5. **Plural-looking but singular:** news, mathematics, physics, economics. The news **is** good.
6. **Two nouns seen as one thing:** Bread and butter **is** my favourite breakfast.
7. **Collective nouns** take a singular verb when the group acts as one unit: The team **is** playing well.
8. **A number of** + plural → plural verb (A number of students **were** absent). **The number of** → singular verb (The number of students **is** increasing).

=== v-voice | Active and Passive Voice
summary: Converting between active and passive across tenses, imperatives, questions and complements.
---
**Active:** the subject does the action (*She writes a letter*).
**Passive:** the subject receives it (*A letter is written by her*).

## How to convert

1. Make the object the new subject.
2. Use the right form of **be** for the tense, plus the **past participle**.
3. Add "by + agent", which can be dropped if the agent is obvious or general ("people", "they").

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Simple present | writes | is written |
| Present continuous | is painting | is being painted |
| Present perfect | has finished | has been finished |
| Simple past | built | was built |
| Future | will announce | will be announced |

## Special cases

- **Imperative:** *Close the door* → *Let the door be closed.*
- **Questions:** *Who wrote this book?* → *By whom was this book written?*
- **Object complement:** *They made him captain* → *He was made captain.*

:::mcq easy
Q: Change to the passive: "She writes a letter."
- A letter was written by her.
- [x] A letter is written by her.
- A letter is being written by her.
- A letter has written by her.
> Simple present passive: is + past participle.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Change to the passive: "They built the bridge."
- The bridge is built by them.
- [x] The bridge was built by them.
- The bridge has been built by them.
- The bridge built by them.
> Simple past passive: was + past participle.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Change to the active: "The cake was eaten by Tom."
- Tom eats the cake.
- [x] Tom ate the cake.
- Tom has eaten the cake.
- The cake ate Tom.
> The agent becomes the subject, and the tense stays the same.
:::

:::mcq medium

> Imperatives become "Let … be + past participle".
:::

=== v-narration | Direct and Indirect Speech
summary: Reporting statements, questions, commands and exclamations, with backshifted tenses, pronouns and time words.
---
## Rules for a past reporting verb (said, asked)

1. **Backshift the tense:** am/is → was, will → would, have done → had done, did → had done.
2. **Change pronouns** to fit the speaker.
3. **Change time and place words:** tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the previous day, now → then, here → there.
4. **Universal truths don't backshift:** The teacher said that the earth **goes** round the sun.

## By sentence type

| Type | Reporting verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Statement | said (that) | He said that he was tired. |
| Yes/no question | asked if/whether | He asked her if she was coming. |
| Wh- question | asked + wh-word + statement order | He asked me where I lived. |
| Request | requested + to | She requested me to help her. |
| Command | told/ordered + (not) to | The teacher told the students not to make a noise. |
| Exclamation | exclaimed that | She exclaimed that it was a lovely day. |

:::mcq easy

- He said that he is tired.

> Backshift is to was, and I becomes he.
:::

:::mcq medium

- [x] She said that she would come the next day.
- She said that I would come tomorrow.
- She said she will come the next day.
> Will becomes would, and tomorrow becomes the next day.
:::

:::mcq medium

=== v-sentence-correction | Sentence Correction
summary: Choosing the grammatically correct sentence: agreement, tense, idiom, redundancy and double negatives.
---
In sentence correction you choose the **one correct version** from several. Most errors come from a short list of traps.

## The error checklist

1. **Subject–verb agreement:** Each of them **has**.
2. **Tense:** a finished time takes the simple past (I **saw** him yesterday).
3. **Idiom:** look forward to **meeting** ("to" here is a preposition, so a gerund follows).
4. **Prepositions:** despite the rain (✘ despite **of**).
5. **Double comparatives:** ✘ more taller → ✔ taller.
6. **Redundancy:** ✘ returned **back**, ✘ repeat **again**, ✘ **revert back**.
7. **Double negatives:** ✘ Unless you **don't** work hard. "Unless" already means "if not".
8. **Relative clause agreement:** one of the best players who **have** ever played (the verb agrees with "players").
9. **Uncountable nouns:** furniture **is**, information **is**.

:::mcq easy
Q: Which sentence is correct?
- He don't like tea.
- [x] He doesn't like tea.
- He not like tea.
- He doesn't likes tea.
> A third-person singular subject takes "doesn't" + base form.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: Which sentence is correct?
- She is one of the best players who has ever played.
- [x] She is one of the best players who have ever played.
- She is one of the best player who have ever played.
- She is one of best players who has ever played.
> "Who" refers to "players" (plural).
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Which sentence is correct?
- I am looking forward to meet you.
- [x] I am looking forward to meeting you.
- I am looking forward for meeting you.
- I look forward meet you.
> In "look forward to", "to" is a preposition, so a gerund follows.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Which sentence is correct?
- The furniture are new.
- [x] The furniture is new.
- The furnitures are new.
- The furnitures is new.
> "Furniture" is uncountable.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Which sentence is correct?
- Despite of the rain, we went out.
- [x] Despite the rain, we went out.
- In despite the rain, we went out.

:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Which sentence is correct?
- He is more taller than me.
- [x] He is taller than me.
- He is most taller than me.
- He is more tall than me.
> Avoid double comparatives.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Which sentence is correct?
- Each of them have a car.
- [x] Each of them has a car.
- Each of them having a car.
- Each of them are having a car.
> "Each" is singular.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Which sentence is correct?
- I have seen him yesterday.
- [x] I saw him yesterday.
- I had seen him yesterday.
- I see him yesterday.
> A finished time takes the simple past.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Which sentence is correct?
- She returned back home.
- [x] She returned home.
- She returned back to home.
- She was returned back home.
> "Returned back" is redundant.
:::

:::mcq medium

=== v-error-spotting | Error Spotting
summary: Finding the part of a sentence that contains an error, or deciding that there is no error.
---
The sentence is split into parts (A), (B), (C), and option (D) is "No error". Find the part with the mistake.

## Strategy

1. Read the whole sentence first for meaning.
2. Find the **subject and main verb**, and check that they agree.
3. Check the **tense signals** (since/for, yesterday) and the **fixed pairs** (prefer … to, senior to, scarcely … when).
4. Check pronouns, articles and prepositions.
5. Choose "No error" only after checking every part; it is right about 10–20% of the time.

## High-frequency errors

- one of my **friends** (plural after "one of")
- **for** five years (not "since" with a length of time)
- prefer A **to** B, senior **to**
- The police **have** (police is always plural)
- where **I was** (indirect questions use statement order)

=== v-spelling | Spelling
summary: Frequently misspelt words, doubled letters and the ie/ei rule.
---
## Patterns

- **i before e, except after c** (when it sounds like "ee"): believe, achieve, but receive, ceiling.
- **Doubled consonants:** accommodate (cc + mm), committee (mm + tt + ee), embarrass (rr + ss), occurrence (cc + rr), millennium (ll + nn).
- **Silent letters and vowels:** rhythm, separate (a-**rat**-e), definitely (de-**finite**-ly).

## Commonly misspelt (correct forms)

accommodate · achieve · committee · conscience · definitely · embarrass · existence · guarantee · independent · millennium · necessary · occurrence · privilege · receive · recommend · rhythm · separate · tomorrow

:::tip
