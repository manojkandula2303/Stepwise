=== v-grammar-fundamentals | Grammar Fundamentals
summary: Subject and predicate, objects, phrases and clauses, and the four kinds and three structures of sentences.
---
## Building blocks

- **Subject:** who or what the sentence is about. *The tall boy in the red shirt* won the race.
- **Predicate:** what is said about the subject. Birds *fly south in winter*.
- **Object:** what receives the action. She wrote *a letter*.

## Phrase vs clause

- A **phrase** has no subject–verb pair: *in the morning*, *a cup of tea*.
- A **clause** has both. An **independent** clause can stand alone (*I left early*); a **dependent** clause cannot (*because I was tired*).

## Four kinds of sentence

| Kind | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Declarative | states | The shop opens at nine. |
| Interrogative | asks | Is the shop open? |
| Imperative | commands or requests | Close the door. |
| Exclamatory | expresses emotion | What a beautiful view! |

## Three structures

- **Simple:** one independent clause. *She sings.*
- **Compound:** two independent clauses joined by and, but, or, so. *I wanted tea, but she ordered coffee.*
- **Complex:** an independent clause plus a dependent clause. *Although it rained, we played.*

A **relative (adjective) clause** describes a noun: The man *who called yesterday* is my uncle.

=== v-parts-of-speech | Parts of Speech
summary: The eight parts of speech, and how one word can change category with its job in a sentence.

| Part | Job | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | names a person, place, thing or idea | Delhi, honesty |
| Pronoun | replaces a noun | he, they, which |
| Verb | action or state | run, is, seem |
| Adjective | describes a noun | beautiful, fast (runner) |
| Adverb | describes a verb, adjective or adverb | quickly, very, fast (runs) |

## Function decides the category

The **same word** can be different parts of speech:
- *light* the candle (verb), a *light* bag (adjective), the *light* (noun)
- He is a *fast* runner (adjective), he runs *fast* (adverb)
- Everyone *but* John came (preposition), I tried, *but* failed (conjunction)
- *Running* is good exercise (a gerund, which acts as a noun)

=== v-nouns | Nouns
summary: Kinds of nouns, countable vs uncountable, irregular plurals, compound plurals and possessives.

## Kinds of nouns

- **Proper:** a specific name (Delhi, Ravi)
- **Common:** a general name (city, boy)
- **Collective:** a group (herd, team, flock)
- **Abstract:** an idea or quality (honesty, freedom)
- **Material:** a substance (gold, water)

## Countable and uncountable

Uncountable nouns take no "a/an" and no plural: **information, advice, furniture, luggage, equipment, news**.
✘ *an information* → ✔ *some information* / *a piece of information*

## Tricky plurals

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| child | children |
| criterion | criteria |
| phenomenon | phenomena |
| analysis | analyses |
| mother-in-law | **mothers**-in-law (pluralise the main word) |

Some nouns are **always plural**: scissors, trousers, spectacles, cattle.

## Possessives

- Singular: the boy's bag
- Plural ending in -s: the boys' bags
- Irregular plural: the **children's** toys

=== v-pronouns | Pronouns
summary: Subject and object case, reflexives, relative pronouns, who/whom and pronoun agreement.
---
## Case

| Subject | Object | Possessive |
|---|---|---|
| I | me | mine |
| he | him | his |
| she | her | hers |
| we | us | ours |
| they | them | theirs |
| who | whom | whose |

- After a preposition, use the **object** form: between you and **me**.
- In comparisons, complete the clause mentally: taller than **I** (am).
- In formal English, "It is **I** who am to blame." The verb agrees with "I".

## Who or whom?

Answer the question with he or him. If the answer is **him**, use **whom**: Whom did you meet? (I met him.)

## Reflexive pronouns

myself, herself, themselves, … are used when the subject and object are the same: She hurt **herself**.

## Agreement

- "Each", "every", "either" and "neither" are singular: Each of the students must bring **his or her** book.
- "One" pairs with "one's": One should respect **one's** elders.

## Relative pronouns

who/whom for people, **which** for things, **that** for either, whose for possession.

=== v-verbs | Verbs
summary: Irregular forms, auxiliaries and modals, transitive vs intransitive, lie/lay, hanged/hung and the subjunctive.
---
## Principal parts

Every verb has a base form, past tense and past participle: write – wrote – **written**, begin – began – **begun**, go – **went** – gone.

## Helping verbs

- **Auxiliaries** (be, have, do) build tenses: She **is** reading.
- **Modals** (can, could, may, might, must, should, would) show ability, permission or obligation: You **must** wear a seatbelt.

## Transitive and intransitive

A transitive verb takes an object (She **kicked** the ball). An intransitive verb doesn't (He **slept**).

## Confusing pairs

| Verb | Meaning | Past | Past participle |
|---|---|---|---|
| lie | recline | **lay** | lain |
| lay | put down | laid | laid |
| hang | suspend an object | hung | hung |
| hang | execute | **hanged** | hanged |

## Subjunctive

Use **were** for imagined or unreal situations: If I **were** you, I would apologise.

=== v-adjectives | Adjectives
summary: Comparison, the royal order of adjectives, few/a few, fewer/less, elder/older and Latin comparatives.

## Degrees of comparison

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| tall | taller | tallest |
| good | **better** | **best** |
| bad | **worse** | **worst** |
| interesting | more interesting | most interesting |

## Order of adjectives

Opinion → size → age → shape → colour → origin → material → purpose
→ *a **beautiful small old** round brown Indian **wooden** jewellery box*

## Commonly confused

- **few** = hardly any (negative): I have *few* friends here, so I feel lonely.
- **a few** = some (positive): I have *a few* friends here.
- **fewer** goes with countable nouns (fewer people); **less** with uncountable nouns (less water).
- **elder** is for family members and comes before the noun (my *elder* brother); **older** is general.
- **Latin comparatives** take "to", not "than": senior **to**, junior **to**, superior **to**, prefer **to**.

=== v-adverbs | Adverbs
summary: Kinds of adverbs, their position, hard/hardly and late/lately, too vs very, and how "only" changes meaning.
---
Adverbs describe verbs, adjectives or other adverbs, answering **how, when, where, how often and to what extent**.

| Kind | Examples |
|---|---|
| Manner | beautifully, quickly |
| Time | now, yesterday, late |
| Place | here, there, everywhere |
| Frequency | always, often, never |
| Degree | very, too, quite |

## Position

Frequency adverbs go **after "be"** and **before the main verb**: He **is always** late. She **always arrives** on time.

## Pairs that change meaning

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| hard | with effort (He works hard.) |
| hardly | barely (I can hardly hear you.) |
| late | not on time (The train arrived late.) |
| lately | recently (I haven't seen him lately.) |

**too** means "more than is wanted" and leads to a negative result: The tea is *too* hot to drink. **very** just intensifies.

## "Only"

Place "only" right next to the word it limits:
- *Only Ravi* saw the film. (nobody else did)
- Ravi *only saw* the film. (he didn't, say, review it)

=== v-conjunctions | Conjunctions
summary: Coordinating and subordinating conjunctions, and correlative pairs such as either–or, not only–but also and no sooner–than.

## Coordinating conjunctions (FANBOYS)

**F**or, **A**nd, **N**or, **B**ut, **O**r, **Y**et, **S**o join equal parts of a sentence.

## Subordinating conjunctions

These introduce a dependent clause: although, because, since, unless, until, while, so that.
*Although* he was tired, he kept working.

## Correlative pairs (always together)

| Pair | Example |
|---|---|
| either … or | Either you or I will go. |
| neither … nor | Neither the manager nor the staff were present. |
| not only … but also | Not only is she smart, but she is also kind. |
| no sooner … **than** | No sooner had she left than the phone rang. |
| hardly / scarcely … **when** | Hardly had he arrived when it started raining. |
| both … and | Both Ram and Shyam came. |

:::warning
"No sooner" always pairs with **than**, never "when". "Hardly" and "scarcely" pair with **when**, never "than".
:::

=== v-articles | Articles
summary: a vs an by sound, uses of "the", and where no article is needed.
---
## A or an: go by the sound, not the spelling

- **an** before a vowel **sound**: an hour, an honest man, an MBA
- **a** before a consonant **sound**: a university (yoo-), a European (yoo-), a one-rupee coin (wun-)

## Use "the" for

- Something unique: the sun, the earth
- Rivers, oceans and mountain ranges: the Ganges, the Himalayas
- Musical instruments: She plays **the** piano
- Superlatives: **the** best student
- Adjectives used as groups: **the** rich, **the** poor
- Something already mentioned or known to both speakers

## No article

- Meals: We had lunch at noon
- Languages and most sports: She speaks French, they play cricket
- Plural or uncountable nouns used in general: Milk is healthy
