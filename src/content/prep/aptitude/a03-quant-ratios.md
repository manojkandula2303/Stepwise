=== q-ratio-proportion | Ratio and Proportion
summary: Combining ratios, dividing amounts, the proportionals and "add the same number" problems.
---
A **ratio** a : b compares two quantities of the same kind. A **proportion** says that two ratios are equal: a : b = c : d, which means ad = bc.

## Combining ratios

To merge A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5, make B the same in both. Multiply the first by 4 and the second by 3: A : B : C = **8 : 12 : 15**.

## Dividing an amount

Divide ₹1200 in the ratio 3 : 5. There are 8 parts, each worth 150, so the shares are 450 and **750**.

If the ratio is given as fractions (1/2 : 1/3 : 1/4), multiply by the LCM of the denominators (12) to get 6 : 4 : 3.

## The proportionals

| Name | Definition | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Fourth proportional to a, b, c | a : b = c : x | x = bc/a |
| Third proportional to a, b | a : b = b : x | x = b²/a |
| Mean proportional of a, b | a : x = x : b | x = √(ab) |

## Adding the same number

**Example.** Two numbers are in the ratio 3 : 5. When 10 is added to each, the ratio becomes 5 : 7.
(3x + 10)/(5x + 10) = 5/7 gives 21x + 70 = 25x + 50, so x = 5. The numbers are **15 and 25**.

## Coin problems

Convert the ratio of **counts** into a ratio of **values**. For ₹1, 50p and 25p coins in the count ratio 5 : 6 : 8, the value ratio is 5 : 3 : 2.

:::mcq easy

=== q-partnership | Partnership
summary: Sharing profit by capital × time, partners joining late or withdrawing, and working-partner salaries.
---
Partners share profit in the ratio of **capital × time** (the "investment-months").

## Same time, different capital

Profit is shared in the ratio of capitals. With 5000 : 7000 = 5 : 7 and a profit of ₹3600, A gets 3600 × 5/12 = **₹1500**.

## Different time

Multiply each capital by its months.

**Example.** A invests ₹6000 for the whole year; B joins after 4 months with ₹8000.
A : B = 6000 × 12 : 8000 × 8 = 72,000 : 64,000 = **9 : 8**.

## Capital that changes mid-year

Add up each segment.

**Example.** A starts with 50,000 and withdraws 10,000 after 4 months. B starts with 40,000 and adds 10,000 after 4 months.
- A = 50,000 × 4 + 40,000 × 8 = 520,000
- B = 40,000 × 4 + 50,000 × 8 = 560,000
- Ratio **13 : 14**

## Working partner

First pay the working partner's salary or commission out of the profit. Split only the remainder by investment.

## Reverse questions

If the profit ratio is p : q and the time ratio is t₁ : t₂, the capital ratio is p/t₁ : q/t₂.

> 3600 × 5/12 = 1500.

=== q-averages | Averages
summary: Mean, adding or removing items, replacing a member, and overlapping groups.
---
**Average = sum of values ÷ number of values.** Most problems are easier if you turn every average back into a **sum** first.

## Shortcuts

- The average of an arithmetic sequence is (first + last)/2.
  - Average of 1 to 20 = **10.5**; average of the odd numbers 1 to 99 = **50**.
- Adding a value equal to the average leaves the average unchanged.

## Adding, removing, replacing

**Removed value** = old sum − new sum.

**Example.** The average age of 30 students is 15. Including the teacher, it becomes 16.
Teacher = 31 × 16 − 30 × 15 = 496 − 450 = **46**.

**Replacement.** When the average of n people rises by d after one person replaces another:
new person = old person + n × d.
For n = 8, d = 2.5 and an old member of 65 kg, the new person weighs 65 + 20 = **85 kg**.

## Overlapping groups

If 11 numbers average 50, the first 6 average 49 and the last 6 average 52, the 6th number is counted twice:
6th = 6 × 49 + 6 × 52 − 11 × 50 = 294 + 312 − 550 = **56**.

## Batting averages

Required score in the next innings = (new average × new innings) − old total.

:::mcq easy
Q: What is the average of 12, 15, 18, 21 and 24?

- [x] 18
- 19
- 20
> These are evenly spaced, so the average is the middle term, 18.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Five numbers average 20. One is removed and the rest average 18. What number was removed?

- [x] 28
- 26
- 30
> 5 × 20 − 4 × 18 = 100 − 72 = 28.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: The average age of 30 students is 15. Including the teacher, the average becomes 16. How old is the teacher?

- [x] 46
- 45
- 40
> 31 × 16 − 30 × 15 = 496 − 450 = 46.
:::

:::mcq easy

> (1 + 20)/2 = 10.5.
:::

:::mcq medium

- [x] 62

> 11 × 42 − 10 × 40 = 462 − 400 = 62.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Eleven numbers average 50. The first six average 49 and the last six average 52. What is the 6th number?

- [x] 56

:::

:::mcq medium

=== q-weighted-averages | Weighted Averages and Alligation
summary: Combining group averages, the alligation cross and average speed.
---
When groups of different sizes are combined, the overall average is a **weighted average**:

**Combined average = (n₁a₁ + n₂a₂ + …) / (n₁ + n₂ + …)**

## Alligation: the shortcut for two groups

To mix two groups with averages a₁ < a₂ and get an overall average m:

**n₁ : n₂ = (a₂ − m) : (m − a₁)**

```
  a₁            a₂
     \        /
        m
     /        \
 a₂ − m      m − a₁
```

**Example.** In what ratio should tea at ₹30 and ₹45 be mixed to sell at ₹40 at cost?
(45 − 40) : (40 − 30) = 5 : 10 = **1 : 2**.

**Example.** Boys average 70 and girls 80; the class averages 74. Boys : girls = (80 − 74) : (74 − 70) = **3 : 2**.

## Average speed

For equal **distances** at speeds u and v, the average speed is **2uv/(u + v)**, not (u + v)/2.
At 40 and 60 km/h: 2 × 40 × 60/100 = **48 km/h**.

For equal **times**, the simple average (u + v)/2 is correct.

:::mcq easy
Q: Class A has 30 students averaging 60 and class B has 20 averaging 70. What is the combined average?

- [x] 64
- 66
- 63
> (1800 + 1400)/50 = 64.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: 20 men average 50 kg and 30 women average 40 kg. What is the overall average weight?
- 45 kg
- [x] 44 kg
- 46 kg
- 42 kg
> (1000 + 1200)/50 = 44.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Rice at ₹40/kg and ₹60/kg is mixed in the ratio 3 : 2. What is the cost of the mixture per kg?
- ₹50
- [x] ₹48
- ₹52
- ₹46
> (3 × 40 + 2 × 60)/5 = 240/5 = 48.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: 10 people average 20. When 15 more join, the 25 people average 26. What is the average of the 15 who joined?

- [x] 30
- 32
- 29
> (650 − 200)/15 = 30.
:::

:::mcq easy

- [x] 1 : 2
- 1 : 3

> (45 − 40) : (40 − 30) = 5 : 10 = 1 : 2.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Boys average 70, girls average 80 and the class averages 74. What is the ratio of boys to girls?

- [x] 3 : 2

=== q-ages | Problems on Ages
summary: Setting up age equations, ratio-of-ages problems and the "intervals" trick.
---
Age problems are linear equations in disguise. Let the unknown present age be x, then translate each sentence into an equation.

## Key facts

- Everyone ages at the same rate, so the **difference** between two people's ages never changes.
- "n years ago" means subtract n from **every** person's age; "after n years" means add n to every age.

## Ratio method

**Example.** Present ages are in the ratio 4 : 5, and six years ago the ratio was 3 : 4.
(4x − 6)/(5x − 6) = 3/4 gives 16x − 24 = 15x − 18, so x = 6. The ages are **24 and 30**.

## The "times" method

**Example.** A father is three times as old as his son. In 15 years he will be twice as old.
3x + 15 = 2(x + 15), so x = **15**. The son is 15 and the father 45.

## Children born at equal intervals

If 5 children are born 3 years apart and their ages add up to 50, then x + (x+3) + (x+6) + (x+9) + (x+12) = 50.
So 5x + 30 = 50, and the youngest is **4**.

:::tip
In the exam, plugging the options back in is often faster than solving: check which option satisfies both conditions.
:::

> 3x + 15 = 2(x + 15), so x = 15.
:::

=== q-mixtures | Mixtures and Alligation
summary: Mixing at a target price or concentration, adding water and the repeated-replacement formula.
---
Mixture problems use two ideas: **alligation** for ratios, and **conservation** of whichever component doesn't change.

## Alligation recap

To mix two items worth c₁ and c₂ and get a mean of m:
**quantity₁ : quantity₂ = (c₂ − m) : (m − c₁)**.
This works for prices, concentrations and profit percentages alike.

**Example.** 1000 kg of sugar is sold partly at 8% profit and partly at 18% profit, for 14% overall.
Ratio = (18 − 14) : (14 − 8) = 4 : 6, so **600 kg** were sold at 18%.

## Add water or add solute: track what stays fixed

**Example.** 40 L of milk and water in the ratio 3 : 1 holds 30 L of milk and 10 L of water. To reach 3 : 2, the water must become 20 L, so add **10 L**.

**Example.** 60 L of 20% alcohol holds 12 L of alcohol. Adding x L of pure alcohol to reach 40%: 12 + x = 0.4(60 + x), so x = **20 L**.

## Repeated replacement

A container holds V litres of pure liquid. Each time, x litres are taken out and replaced with water. After n operations:

**pure liquid left = V × (1 − x/V)ⁿ**

**Example.** 80 L of milk; 8 L replaced twice gives 80 × (0.9)² = **64.8 L**.

## Selling water at the price of milk

If a milkman sells the mixture at cost price and gains g%, water : milk = g : 100. A 25% gain means **1 : 4**.
