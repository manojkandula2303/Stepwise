=== v-fill-blanks | Fill in the Blanks
summary: Choosing the word that fits both the grammar and the meaning of a sentence.
---
A blank must fit **grammatically** (the right part of speech and form) and **logically** (the meaning and tone of the sentence).

## Method

1. Read the whole sentence and predict a word **before** looking at the options.
2. Look for **signal words**:
   - Contrast: although, despite, but, yet → the blank opposes the other half.
   - Cause: because, so, therefore → the blank agrees with it.
   - Definition: a semicolon or colon often explains the blank.
3. Eliminate options with the wrong grammar, then the wrong tone.

## Commonly confused words

- **effect** (noun, the result) vs **affect** (verb, to influence)
- **flair** (talent) vs **flare** (a burst of flame)
- **compliment** (praise) vs **complement** (complete)
- **principal** (head, or main) vs **principle** (a rule)

:::mcq easy
Q: "After a long debate, the committee ___ its decision."
- announce
- [x] announced
- announcing
- announcement
> A past-tense verb is needed.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: "She was ___ by the beauty of the valley."
- bored
- [x] enchanted
- irritated
- confused
> The tone is positive: delight.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: "His ___ attitude annoyed everyone in the meeting."
- humble
- [x] arrogant
- polite
- generous
> Only a negative trait would annoy people.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: "We must ___ water during the summer months."
- waste
- [x] conserve
- pollute
- spill
> "Must" plus a summer shortage implies saving.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: "The thief was caught ___-handed."
- blue
- [x] red
- black
- white
> The idiom is "caught red-handed".
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "Despite his great wealth, he lived a ___ life."
- lavish
- [x] frugal
- luxurious
- extravagant
> "Despite" signals a contrast with wealth.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "The medicine had an immediate ___ on the patient."
- affect
- [x] effect
- affection
- efect
> A noun meaning "result" is needed: effect.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "She has a ___ for languages; she speaks five of them."
- flare
- [x] flair
- fear
- flavour
> "Flair" means natural talent.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "The principal ___ the students for their excellent results."
- condemned
- [x] commended
- commanded
- comprehended
> "Commended" means praised.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: "The evidence was so ___ that the jury reached a verdict within minutes."
- tenuous
- [x] compelling
- ambiguous
- scanty
> A quick verdict implies strong, convincing evidence.
:::

=== v-cloze-test | Cloze Test
summary: Filling numbered blanks in a passage using grammar, collocation and the flow of the argument.
---
A cloze test is a passage with several numbered blanks. Each blank depends on the **whole passage**, not just its own sentence.

## Method

1. **Skim the passage first** for its topic and tone; don't fill blanks on the first read.
2. For each blank, check:
   - **Grammar:** part of speech, tense, singular or plural.
   - **Collocation:** words that naturally go together (*maintain* a weight, *stick to* a routine).
   - **Logic:** connectors (yet, since, not) and the argument's direction.
3. Re-read the completed passage to confirm it flows.

:::context
**Passage**

Regular exercise is one of the most **(1)** ___ habits a person can develop. It **(2)** ___ the heart, improves mood and helps **(3)** ___ a healthy weight. Yet many people find it hard to **(4)** ___ a routine. The key is to start small: a brisk twenty-minute walk is far better **(5)** ___ no exercise at all. Over time, as stamina **(6)** ___, the duration can be increased. Choosing an activity you **(7)** ___ makes it easier to stay consistent. Exercising with a friend also adds **(8)** ___, since you are less likely to skip a session when someone is waiting for you. **(9)** ___ all, the best exercise is the one you will actually do, **(10)** ___ the most fashionable one.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Blank (1)
- harmful
- [x] beneficial
- boring
- expensive
> The passage praises exercise.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Blank (2)
- weakens
- [x] strengthens
- stops
- ignores
> It must be a positive effect on the heart, in the present tense.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Blank (3)
- lose
- [x] maintain
- gaining
- reduce of
> The collocation is "maintain a healthy weight"; "helps" is followed by the base form.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Blank (4)
- stick
- [x] stick to
- stuck to
- sticking
> The phrasal verb is "stick to a routine", after "to" (infinitive).
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Blank (5)
- then
- [x] than
- as
- from
> A comparison uses "better than".
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Blank (6)
- falls
- [x] builds
- ends
- stops
> Stamina builds up over time, which lets you exercise longer.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Blank (7)
- hate
- [x] enjoy
- avoid
- fear
> Enjoyment helps consistency.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Blank (8)
- confusion
- [x] accountability
- pressure-free
- distance
> Someone waiting for you makes you answerable.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Blank (9)
- Over
- [x] Above
- Before
- Under
> The idiom is "above all".
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: Blank (10)
- and
- [x] not
- also
- even
> The sentence contrasts "the one you will do" with "the most fashionable one".
:::

=== v-sentence-completion | Sentence Completion
summary: One- and two-blank sentences solved with signal words, contrast and cause.
---
These are fill-in-the-blank questions with **two blanks** or **subtler vocabulary**. The structure of the sentence tells you what the answer must do.

| Signal | Relationship | Example |
|---|---|---|
| although, despite, far from, yet | **contrast** | *Far from being ___, the plan was carefully thought out* → haphazard |
| because, so, since, therefore | **cause** | *Because the instructions were ___, many filled the form incorrectly* → ambiguous |
| a semicolon or colon | **explanation** | *She is known for her ___; she never reveals secrets* → discretion |
| and, also | **continuation** | the blanks agree in tone |

**Two blanks:** solve the easier blank first, eliminate options, then check the other blank against the options left.

:::mcq medium
Q: "Although the task was ___, she completed it ___."
- simple, easily
- [x] difficult, easily
- difficult, slowly
- easy, quickly
> "Although" needs a contrast: difficult, yet done with ease.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: "The speaker was so ___ that half the audience ___ asleep."
- engaging, fell
- [x] boring, fell
- boring, stayed
- loud, fell
> "So … that" shows cause and effect.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "His argument was ___; nobody could find a single flaw in it."
- weak
- [x] watertight
- confusing
- emotional
> The semicolon explains the blank: no flaws.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "Because the instructions were ___, many people ___ the form incorrectly."
- clear, filled
- [x] ambiguous, filled
- ambiguous, submitted correctly
- simple, completed
> Unclear instructions cause mistakes.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "She is known for her ___; she never reveals a secret."
- curiosity
- [x] discretion
- honesty
- talkativeness
> Discretion means keeping things private.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "The new policy was ___ by the public, who protested against it for weeks."
- welcomed
- [x] resented
- ignored
- praised
> The protests show opposition.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "Far from being ___, the plan had been carefully thought out."
- detailed
- [x] haphazard
- logical
- precise
> "Far from" sets up the opposite of "carefully thought out".
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: "The scientist's claims were ___ by later experiments, which proved them correct."
- refuted
- [x] vindicated
- questioned
- abandoned
> "Vindicated" means shown to be right.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: "In spite of repeated failures, he remained ___."
- hopeless
- [x] optimistic
- defeated
- bitter
> "In spite of" sets up a contrast with failure.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: "The critic's review was ___: it praised the acting but ___ the script."
- glowing, admired
- [x] mixed, criticised
- harsh, praised
- mixed, celebrated
> "Praised … but" means the review had both good and bad points.
:::

=== v-paragraph-completion | Paragraph Completion
summary: Choosing the sentence that best concludes or continues a short paragraph.
---
You get a short paragraph and must choose the sentence that **best completes it**.

## What the right answer does

- **Continues the logical direction.** If the last line raises a problem ("However, they are expensive"), the conclusion often addresses it.
- **Stays in scope.** It doesn't introduce a new topic or an extreme claim.
- **Matches the tone.** A neutral paragraph needs a neutral ending; a balanced debate needs a balanced conclusion.
- **Links back.** It often uses "therefore", "as a result" or "though" to connect to the ideas before it.

:::warning
Reject options that are true but unrelated, or that contradict the paragraph's direction.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "Many cities are adopting electric buses. They produce no tailpipe emissions and are quieter than diesel buses. However, they are expensive to buy. ___"
- Diesel buses are popular in villages.
- [x] Over their lifetime, though, lower fuel and maintenance costs can offset the higher price.
- Therefore, cities should stop buying buses.
- Electric cars are also quiet.
> It answers the cost concern raised by "However".
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: "Reading before bed helps many people relax. Unlike screens, a printed book does not emit blue light. ___"
- Books are expensive.
- [x] As a result, readers often fall asleep more easily.
- Screens are useful for work.
- Libraries close early.
> It completes the cause–effect chain about sleep.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: "The company's sales fell for three quarters in a row. Customers complained about slow deliveries. ___"
- The company changed its logo.
- [x] To win them back, it invested in a faster delivery network.
- Many people like online shopping.
- Sales always fall in winter.
> It addresses the complaint that was mentioned.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: "Bees play a vital role in agriculture. They pollinate many crops that humans depend on. ___"
- Bees can sting.
- [x] A decline in bee populations could therefore threaten food supplies.
- Honey is sweet.
- Some crops grow in water.
> It draws the logical consequence.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "Learning a second language has benefits beyond travel. Studies link it to better memory and multitasking. ___"
- English is widely spoken.
- [x] It may even delay the onset of age-related cognitive decline.
- Travel is expensive.
- Some languages are hard to learn.
> It continues the list of cognitive benefits.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "Remote work saves commuting time. But it can blur the line between work and personal life. ___"
- Offices have cafeterias.
- [x] Setting fixed working hours can help maintain that boundary.
- Commuting is tiring.
- Everyone should work from home forever.
> It offers a remedy for the problem just raised.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "Plastic is cheap and durable. Yet these very qualities make it a long-lasting pollutant. ___"
- Plastic comes in many colours.
- [x] A single bottle can take centuries to break down.
- Plastic is used in cars.
- Metal is also durable.
> It illustrates durability as pollution.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: "The museum introduced free entry on Sundays. Visitor numbers doubled within a month. ___"
- Museums store old objects.
- [x] Encouraged, the board is now considering extending the scheme to weekdays.
- Sunday is a holiday.
- Some people dislike museums.
> It is the natural next step after the success.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: "Sleep is when the body repairs itself. Chronic lack of sleep weakens the immune system. ___"
- Some people snore.
- [x] Adults are therefore advised to get seven to nine hours a night.
- Beds can be expensive.
- Coffee keeps people awake.
> It concludes with practical advice.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: "Some argue that standardised tests measure only test-taking skill. Others say they provide a common yardstick across schools. ___"
- Clearly, all tests should be abolished.
- Tests are the only fair measure.
- [x] At heart, the debate is about what we want tests to measure.
- Many students dislike tests.
> A balanced paragraph needs a balanced conclusion; the other options take sides or drift off topic.
:::

=== v-para-jumbles | Para Jumbles
summary: Ordering jumbled sentences into a coherent paragraph using openers, connectors and time sequence.
---
Arrange sentences P, Q, R and S into a logical paragraph.

## Clues

1. **Opening sentence:** introduces the topic or a person by full name, and doesn't start with a pronoun or connector (he, this, however, then).
2. **Pronouns and "this/that":** they point back to something already mentioned, so they come **after** it.
3. **Connectors:** "however" (contrast), "as a result" and "therefore" (consequence), "first … then … finally" (sequence).
4. **Chronology:** past → present → future; cause → effect.
5. **Mandatory pairs:** find two sentences that must be adjacent and build around them.

:::tip
Use the options. Once you've found the opener and one sure pair, usually only one option fits.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: P: He opened the door slowly. Q: The room was dark and silent. R: Ravi reached home late at night. S: He switched on the light and gasped.
- PRQS
- [x] RPQS
- RQPS
- QRPS
> R introduces Ravi, then "He" opens the door, sees the dark room, and switches on the light.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: P: Then add the tea leaves and let it simmer. Q: First, boil a cup of water. R: Finally, strain and serve. S: Add milk and sugar to taste.
- QSPR
- [x] QPSR
- PQSR
- QPRS
> First → then → add milk → finally.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: P: As a result, the crops failed. Q: There was no rain that year. R: Farmers suffered heavy losses. S: The government announced relief.
- QRPS
- [x] QPRS
- PQRS
- QPSR
> Cause → crop failure → losses → relief.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: P: It was invented in the 19th century. Q: The telephone changed communication forever. R: Today, billions use mobile phones. S: Early models needed operators to connect calls.
- QSPR
- [x] QPSR
- PQSR
- QRPS
> Topic → invention → early models → today.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: P: However, it is also a source of stress. Q: Social media connects people across the world. R: Users often compare themselves with others. S: Experts advise limiting screen time.
- QRPS
- [x] QPRS
- PQRS
- QPSR
> Benefit → "However" → why it causes stress → advice.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: P: She practised every day for years. Q: Meera dreamed of becoming a pianist. R: Last month, she performed at a national concert. S: The audience gave her a standing ovation.
- QRPS
- [x] QPRS
- PQRS
- QPSR
> Dream → practice → performance → reaction.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: P: This saves both time and fuel. Q: Many offices now allow staff to work from home. R: Employees no longer need to commute every day. S: Surprisingly, productivity has not dropped.
- QPRS
- [x] QRPS
- RQPS
- QRSP
> "This" in P refers to not commuting (R).
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: P: He apologised immediately. Q: The waiter spilled soup on a customer. R: The manager offered a free dessert. S: The customer accepted with a smile.
- QRPS
- [x] QPRS
- PQRS
- QSPR
> Incident → apology → compensation → reaction.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: P: Water is heated in a boiler. Q: The steam turns a turbine. R: The turbine drives a generator that produces electricity. S: This produces high-pressure steam.
- PQSR
- [x] PSQR
- SPQR
- PSRQ
> Heating → steam → turbine → generator.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: P: Yet few of them check the source. Q: Most people now get their news online. R: This allows false stories to spread quickly. S: Fact-checking websites try to counter this.
- QRPS
- [x] QPRS
- PQRS
- QPSR
> Fact → "Yet" → consequence ("This") → response ("counter this").
:::

=== v-sentence-rearrangement | Sentence Rearrangement
summary: Putting jumbled parts of one sentence into the correct grammatical order.
---
Parts of a single sentence (P, Q, R, S) are jumbled. Put them back in order.

## Method

1. **Find the subject**, usually a noun phrase without a preposition: *the match*, *my mother*.
2. **Attach the verb** straight after it.
3. Add the object or complement, then phrases of **place → manner → time** (time usually comes last).
4. Use **fixed structures**: if … will; not only … but also; no sooner had … than.

**Example.** (P) to the market (Q) went (R) my mother (S) yesterday → R Q P S: *My mother went to the market yesterday.*

:::mcq easy
Q: Arrange: (P) to the market (Q) went (R) my mother (S) yesterday
- QRPS
- [x] RQPS
- RPQS
- PRQS
> Subject → verb → place → time.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Arrange: (P) has been (Q) the project (R) completed (S) successfully
- PQRS
- [x] QPRS
- QRPS
- RQPS
> "The project has been completed successfully."
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Arrange: (P) if you (Q) you will pass (R) study hard (S) the exam
- PQRS
- [x] PRQS
- RPQS
- QSPR
> "If you study hard, you will pass the exam."
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Arrange: (P) a cup of tea (Q) every morning (R) she drinks (S) without sugar
- RPQS
- [x] RPSQ
- PRSQ
- RQPS
> "She drinks a cup of tea without sugar every morning." Time comes last.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Arrange: (P) the book (Q) that I lent you (R) please return (S) by Friday
- PRQS
- [x] RPQS
- RQPS
- PQRS
> "Please return the book that I lent you by Friday."
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Arrange: (P) was cancelled (Q) due to (R) the match (S) heavy rain
- PRQS
- [x] RPQS
- RQSP
- QSRP
> "The match was cancelled due to heavy rain."
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Arrange: (P) not only (Q) she is (R) but also kind (S) intelligent
- PQSR
- [x] QPSR
- QSPR
- QPRS
> "She is not only intelligent but also kind."
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Arrange: (P) the old man (Q) slowly (R) across the road (S) walked
- PQSR
- [x] PSQR
- PSRQ
- SPQR
> "The old man walked slowly across the road." Manner comes before place here.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Arrange: (P) because (Q) he missed the bus (R) he woke up late (S) this morning
- QPSR
- [x] QPRS
- RPQS
- PRQS
> "He missed the bus because he woke up late this morning."
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: Arrange: (P) no sooner (Q) than the lights went out (R) had we sat down (S) to dinner
- PQRS
- [x] PRSQ
- RSPQ
- PSRQ
> "No sooner had we sat down to dinner than the lights went out."
:::
