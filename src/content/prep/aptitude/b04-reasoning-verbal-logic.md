=== r-syllogisms | Syllogisms
summary: Venn-diagram method for All/Some/No statements, definite conclusions, possibilities and either–or cases.
---
A syllogism gives you statements (take them as true even if they sound absurd) and asks which **conclusions must follow**.

## The four statement types

| Statement | Venn picture |
|---|---|
| All A are B | circle A inside circle B |
| Some A are B | the circles overlap |
| No A is B | separate circles |
| Some A are not B | part of A lies outside B |

## Rules that settle most questions

- **All A are B** ⇒ Some A are B, and Some B are A. It does *not* give All B are A.
- **Some A are B** ⇒ Some B are A.
- **No A is B** ⇒ No B is A, and Some A are not B.
- Two **particular** (Some) statements together give **no definite conclusion** linking their ends.
- All A are B + All B are C ⇒ **All A are C**.
- All A are B + No B is C ⇒ **No A is C**.
- Some A are B + All B are C ⇒ **Some A are C**.

## Possibility questions

"Some A being C **is a possibility**" is true whenever some valid diagram allows it, i.e. whenever it isn't explicitly ruled out.

## Either–or

If two conclusions are about the same two terms, neither follows on its own, and together they cover every case (Some A are B / No A is B), then the answer is **either I or II follows**.

:::mcq easy
Q: Statements: All cats are animals. All animals are living beings. Conclusion: All cats are living beings. Does it follow?
- [x] Yes
- No
- Only if some animals are cats
- Cannot be determined
> All A ⊂ B and All B ⊂ C give All A ⊂ C.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Statements: All pens are blue. Conclusions: I. Some pens are blue. II. All blue things are pens.
- Both follow
- [x] Only I follows
- Only II follows
- Neither follows
> "All A are B" gives "Some A are B", but not "All B are A".
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statements: Some books are pens. Some pens are tables. Conclusion: Some books are tables.
- Follows
- [x] Does not follow
- Follows only as a possibility being false
- Follows definitely
> Two "some" statements give no definite link between books and tables.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statements: All roses are flowers. No flower is a tree. Conclusion: No rose is a tree.
- [x] Follows
- Does not follow
- Only some roses are not trees
- Cannot say
> All A are B and No B is C give No A is C.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statements: Some students are players. All players are athletes. Conclusion: Some students are athletes.
- [x] Follows
- Does not follow
- Only if all students are players
- Cannot say
> The students who are players are inside athletes, so some students are athletes.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statements: No car is a bus. All buses are vehicles. Conclusions: I. No car is a vehicle. II. Some vehicles are buses.
- Only I follows
- [x] Only II follows
- Both follow
- Neither follows
> "All buses are vehicles" gives "Some vehicles are buses". Cars could still be vehicles, so I does not follow.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: Statements: Some pens are pencils. No pencil is an eraser. Conclusions: I. Some pens are erasers. II. No pen is an eraser.
- Only I follows
- Only II follows
- [x] Either I or II follows
- Neither follows
> Both are about pens and erasers, neither is definite, and together they cover every case, so it's either I or II.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statements: All doctors are graduates. Some graduates are teachers. Conclusion: Some doctors being teachers is a possibility.
- [x] Follows
- Does not follow
- Follows definitely, not as a possibility
- Cannot say
> Nothing rules it out, so the possibility holds.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statements: No apple is a mango. Conclusion: Some apples are not mangoes.
- [x] Follows
- Does not follow
- Only if there are apples
- Cannot say
> "No A is B" gives "Some A are not B" (assuming A exists, as aptitude tests do).
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: Statements: All A are B. Some B are C. No C is D. Conclusions: I. Some B are not D. II. Some A are C.
- Both follow
- [x] Only I follows
- Only II follows
- Neither follows
> The B's that are C cannot be D, so some B are not D. A–C is never forced, so II does not follow.
:::

=== r-statement-conclusions | Statements and Conclusions
summary: Deciding whether a conclusion follows directly from the given statement, without outside assumptions.
---
A **conclusion follows** only if it can be **directly inferred** from the statement, using no outside knowledge beyond plain common sense.

## Reject conclusions that

- Use **extreme words** the statement doesn't support: *only, all, always, never, definitely, best*.
- Bring in **new information** or new causes.
- Predict outcomes the statement doesn't imply.

## Accept conclusions that

- Restate or directly follow from the statement's claim.
- Are the obvious intent of an advertisement, notice or appeal.

**Example.** Statement: "The government has decided to open 100 new schools in rural areas."
- I. There is a shortage of schools in rural areas. **Follows**; that is why you'd open them.
- II. Urban areas have enough schools. **Doesn't follow**; the statement says nothing about cities.

:::mcq easy
Q: Statement: "Use our toothpaste for whiter teeth in 7 days." Conclusion: People want whiter teeth.
- [x] Follows
- Does not follow
- Follows only for some people
- Cannot be determined
> The advertisement assumes and targets that desire.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Statement: "All employees must attend the safety training on Monday." Conclusion: The training will definitely reduce accidents.
- Follows
- [x] Does not follow
- Follows partly
- Follows only for new employees
> The statement makes attendance compulsory; it says nothing about the outcome.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statement: "The city has banned plastic bags from next month." Conclusions: I. Plastic bags are seen as harmful. II. No plastic will be used in the city.
- Both follow
- [x] Only I follows
- Only II follows
- Neither follows
> The ban implies harm. II overreaches: only bags are banned.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statement: "Only graduates may apply for the post." Conclusions: I. Non-graduates are not eligible. II. All graduates will be selected.
- Both follow
- [x] Only I follows
- Only II follows
- Neither follows
> Eligibility is not selection.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statement: "Train services were cancelled due to heavy rain." Conclusions: I. Heavy rain can disrupt train services. II. Trains never run in the rain.
- Both follow
- [x] Only I follows
- Only II follows
- Neither follows
> "Never" is an unsupported extreme.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statement: "Sales of electric scooters doubled this year." Conclusions: I. More people bought electric scooters this year. II. Petrol scooters were banned.
- Both follow
- [x] Only I follows
- Only II follows
- Neither follows
> II introduces a new cause.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statement: "The library will remain closed on Sundays." Conclusions: I. The library is open on the other days. II. The library staff work on Sundays.
- Only II follows
- [x] Neither follows
- Both follow
- Only I follows
> The statement fixes only Sunday. Other days could have their own closures, and nothing is said about staff.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Statement: "Please switch off the lights when you leave the room." Conclusion: Saving electricity is desirable.
- [x] Follows
- Does not follow
- Partly follows
- Cannot be determined
> That is the evident purpose of the request.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: Statement: "Most students who attended the extra classes scored above 80%." Conclusions: I. Extra classes may help improve scores. II. Every student who scored above 80% attended the extra classes.
- Both follow
- [x] Only I follows
- Only II follows
- Neither follows
> "May help" is a cautious, supported inference. II reverses the relationship and turns "most" into "every".
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statement: "The company has recorded losses for three years in a row." Conclusions: I. The company is facing financial trouble. II. The company will shut down next year.
- Both follow
- [x] Only I follows
- Only II follows
- Neither follows
> Three years of losses indicate trouble, but a shutdown is speculation.
:::

=== r-statement-assumptions | Statements and Assumptions
summary: Identifying the unstated premise the speaker must take for granted for the statement to make sense.
---
An **assumption** is something the speaker **takes for granted** before saying the statement. It is never stated, but without it the statement would make no sense.

## The negation test

Negate the candidate assumption. If the statement then becomes **pointless or contradictory**, it was an assumption.

**Example.** "Join our coaching centre to crack the exam."
- Assumption: *Coaching helps people crack the exam.* Negated: "Coaching doesn't help", which makes the ad pointless. So it **is implicit**.
- Not an assumption: *Other coaching centres are bad.* The ad works fine without it.

## Typical implicit assumptions

- Advertisements assume people respond to ads and want the product.
- Notices and rules assume people will read and follow them.
- Plans and suggestions assume they are feasible and will achieve their aim.

:::warning
Assumptions are modest. Options with "only", "all" or "best" are rarely implicit.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Statement: "Carry an umbrella; it may rain today." Assumption: An umbrella protects you from rain.
- [x] Implicit
- Not implicit
- Partly implicit
- Cannot be determined
> Without it, the advice makes no sense.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Statement: "Our restaurant serves the best biryani in town." Assumption: People like biryani.
- [x] Implicit
- Not implicit
- Implicit only for regular customers
- Cannot be determined
> Advertising biryani assumes there are people who want it.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statement: "The school will hold extra classes for weak students." Assumptions: I. Extra classes can help weak students. II. All students are weak.
- Both are implicit
- [x] Only I is implicit
- Only II is implicit
- Neither is implicit
> II contradicts the targeting of "weak students".
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statement: "Please submit the form before Friday to avoid a late fee." Assumptions: I. People want to avoid the late fee. II. The office is closed on Friday.
- Both are implicit
- [x] Only I is implicit
- Only II is implicit
- Neither is implicit
> The deadline doesn't imply anything about closure.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statement: "The government should build more flyovers to reduce traffic jams." Assumptions: I. Flyovers can reduce traffic jams. II. There are traffic jams now.
- Only I is implicit
- Only II is implicit
- [x] Both are implicit
- Neither is implicit
> The proposal presumes both a problem and that the remedy works.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statement: "If you want good marks, study regularly." Assumptions: I. Regular study leads to good marks. II. Everyone wants good marks.
- Both are implicit
- [x] Only I is implicit
- Only II is implicit
- Neither is implicit
> The "if" clause shows the speaker doesn't assume everyone wants good marks.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statement: "The company has launched an online customer-support chat." Assumption: Customers use the internet.
- [x] Implicit
- Not implicit
- Partly implicit
- Cannot be determined
> An online channel is only useful if customers can get online.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: Statement: "Due to rising fuel prices, the airline has increased its fares by 10%." Assumptions: I. Fuel costs affect the airline's expenses. II. Passengers will stop flying.
- Both are implicit
- [x] Only I is implicit
- Only II is implicit
- Neither is implicit
> The airline raises fares expecting to keep passengers, not lose them.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Statement: "Keep medicines out of children's reach." Assumption: Medicines can be harmful to children.
- [x] Implicit
- Not implicit
- Implicit only for some medicines
- Cannot be determined
> That is the reason behind the warning.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Statement: "Book your tickets early; seats are limited." Assumptions: I. The demand for tickets may exceed the seats. II. Late bookers will pay more.
- Both are implicit
- [x] Only I is implicit
- Only II is implicit
- Neither is implicit
> Pricing isn't mentioned.
:::

=== r-statement-arguments | Statements and Arguments
summary: Judging whether an argument for or against a proposal is strong (relevant, important, valid) or weak.
---
You get a proposal ("Should X be done?") and arguments for and against. Decide whether each argument is **strong** or **weak**.

## A strong argument is

- **Directly relevant** to the question
- About an **important**, significant aspect (not trivial)
- **Logically valid**, supported by fact or plain common sense
- Neither extreme nor universal

## A weak argument is

- Ambiguous, trivial or emotional
- Based on a sweeping generalisation ("all politicians are corrupt")
- A matter of style rather than substance
- A question back, or a restatement of the proposal

**Example.** "Should smoking be banned in public places?"
- *Yes, passive smoking harms non-smokers.* **Strong**: relevant, important, factual.
- *No, smokers will feel bad.* **Weak**: trivial compared with the health stakes.

:::mcq easy
Q: Should helmets be compulsory for two-wheeler riders? Argument: "Yes, helmets greatly reduce head injuries in accidents."
- [x] Strong
- Weak
- Strong only for long journeys
- Irrelevant
> It is relevant, important and factual.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Should helmets be compulsory for two-wheeler riders? Argument: "No, helmets spoil hairstyles."
- Strong
- [x] Weak
- Strong for young riders
- Partly strong
> Appearance is trivial compared with safety.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Should exams be abolished in schools? Argument: "Yes, all students hate exams."
- Strong
- [x] Weak
- Strong if most students agree
- Neutral
> It is a sweeping generalisation, and dislike isn't a reason to abolish assessment.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Should exams be abolished in schools? Argument: "No, exams provide a standard measure of learning."
- [x] Strong
- Weak
- Weak because it's an opinion
- Irrelevant
> It addresses the core purpose of exams.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Should cities ban private cars in the centre? Argument: "Yes, it will reduce pollution and congestion."
- [x] Strong
- Weak
- Weak because it is speculative
- Irrelevant
> It gives significant, directly relevant benefits.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Should cities ban private cars in the centre? Argument: "No, other cities haven't done it."
- Strong
- [x] Weak
- Strong because it shows the norm
- Neutral
> Copying others or not doesn't address the merits.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Should junk food be banned in school canteens? Argument: "Yes, it contributes to childhood obesity."
- [x] Strong
- Weak
- Weak because not all students are obese
- Irrelevant
> It is a relevant, important health concern.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Should junk food be banned in school canteens? Argument: "No, students like junk food."
- Strong
- [x] Weak
- Strong because it shows demand
- Partly strong
> A preference doesn't outweigh health.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: Should working from home be allowed permanently? Argument: "Yes, it saves commuting time and can improve productivity for many roles."
- [x] Strong
- Weak
- Weak because it isn't true for all roles
- Irrelevant
> It is relevant, significant and suitably qualified ("many roles").
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: Should working from home be allowed permanently? Argument: "No, offices were built for a reason."
- Strong
- [x] Weak
- Strong because it uses existing investment
- Neutral
> It is vague and gives no concrete reason.
:::

=== r-cause-effect | Cause and Effect
summary: Deciding whether one event causes the other, whether both are effects of a common cause, or whether they are independent.
---
You get two statements and must decide how they're linked.

| Option | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A is the cause, B is the effect | A happened first and led to B |
| B is the cause, A is the effect | the reverse |
| Both are independent causes | unrelated events |
| Both are effects of independent causes | each has its own separate cause |
| Both are effects of a common cause | something else caused both |

## Approach

1. Ask: which event could **explain** the other?
2. Check the timing: a cause comes before its effect.
3. If neither explains the other but both point to a hidden event (such as heavy rain), choose **common cause**.

**Example.** A: "Schools in the city were closed today." B: "Many roads were flooded." Both are effects of a **common cause** (heavy rain); neither explains the other on its own. If B said "Heavy rain fell overnight", then B would be the cause and A the effect.

:::mcq easy
Q: A: "Heavy rain fell all night." B: "Many low-lying areas were flooded this morning."
- [x] A is the cause and B is the effect
- B is the cause and A is the effect
- Both are independent causes
- Both are effects of a common cause
> Rain overnight explains the flooding.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: A: "The company announced record profits." B: "The company's share price rose sharply."
- [x] A is the cause and B is the effect
- B is the cause and A is the effect
- Both are effects of a common cause
- Both are independent causes
> Good results push the share price up.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A: "Schools in the city were closed today." B: "Several trains were cancelled today."
- A is the cause and B is the effect
- B is the cause and A is the effect
- [x] Both are effects of a common cause
- Both are independent causes
> Something like a storm or a strike explains both.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A: "Vegetable prices have shot up." B: "Unseasonal rain destroyed crops in the region."
- A is the cause and B is the effect
- [x] B is the cause and A is the effect
- Both are independent causes
- Both are effects of a common cause
> The crop damage reduced supply, which raised prices.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A: "The government increased tax on cigarettes." B: "A new cricket stadium opened in the city."
- A is the cause and B is the effect
- B is the cause and A is the effect
- [x] Both are independent causes
- Both are effects of a common cause
> They are unrelated events.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A: "Many employees resigned from the firm." B: "The firm cut salaries by 20%."
- A is the cause and B is the effect
- [x] B is the cause and A is the effect
- Both are effects of a common cause
- Both are independent causes
> Salary cuts plausibly led to the resignations.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: A: "Sales of umbrellas rose this month." B: "Sales of raincoats rose this month."
- A is the cause and B is the effect
- B is the cause and A is the effect
- [x] Both are effects of a common cause
- Both are independent causes
> The monsoon drives both.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A: "The bridge collapsed." B: "Traffic was diverted to another route."
- [x] A is the cause and B is the effect
- B is the cause and A is the effect
- Both are effects of a common cause
- Both are independent causes
> The collapse made the diversion necessary.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: A: "Hospital admissions for respiratory illness increased." B: "Air quality in the city fell to 'severe'."
- A is the cause and B is the effect
- [x] B is the cause and A is the effect
- Both are independent causes
- Both are effects of a common cause
> Poor air quality leads to respiratory illness.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A: "The famous singer cancelled the concert." B: "Ticket holders were offered full refunds."
- [x] A is the cause and B is the effect
- B is the cause and A is the effect
- Both are effects of a common cause
- Both are independent causes
> The refunds follow from the cancellation.
:::

=== r-course-of-action | Course of Action
summary: Choosing practical, reasonable follow-up actions that address a stated problem.
---
You get a situation and proposed **courses of action**. An action **follows** if it is a practical, reasonable step that **solves or reduces the problem**, or deals with it in the near term.

## Good actions

- Address the problem directly (short-term relief or a long-term fix)
- Are feasible and proportionate
- Are taken by the appropriate authority

## Poor actions

- Extreme or drastic responses (banning, shutting down, punishing everyone)
- Irrelevant or impractical steps
- Actions that create bigger problems than they solve

**Example.** "Several cases of food poisoning were reported after a wedding."
- I. The health department should test samples from the caterer. **Follows**.
- II. All weddings should be banned. **Doesn't follow**: extreme.

:::mcq easy
Q: Problem: "Many students failed the maths exam." Action: The school should arrange remedial classes in maths.
- [x] Follows
- Does not follow
- Follows only if parents agree
- Cannot be determined
> It is a direct, practical remedy.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Problem: "Many students failed the maths exam." Action: Maths should be removed from the syllabus.
- Follows
- [x] Does not follow
- Follows for weaker students
- Partly follows
> That is extreme and avoids the problem rather than solving it.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Problem: "Water-logging occurs on the main road every monsoon." Actions: I. Improve the drainage system on that road. II. Close the road permanently.
- Both follow
- [x] Only I follows
- Only II follows
- Neither follows
> II is drastic and impractical.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Problem: "A factory is discharging untreated waste into a river." Actions: I. The pollution board should inspect and order treatment of the waste. II. All factories in the state should be shut down.
- Both follow
- [x] Only I follows
- Only II follows
- Neither follows
> I is proportionate and targeted.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Problem: "There has been a sudden rise in road accidents at a particular junction." Actions: I. Install traffic lights and speed breakers there. II. Study the causes of the accidents at the junction.
- Only I follows
- Only II follows
- [x] Both follow
- Neither follows
> Both the immediate fix and the investigation are reasonable.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Problem: "Customers complain of long waits at a bank branch." Actions: I. Open more counters at peak hours. II. Ask customers to stop visiting the branch.
- Both follow
- [x] Only I follows
- Only II follows
- Neither follows
> II is impractical.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Problem: "A virus outbreak has been reported in a village." Actions: I. Send a medical team to the village immediately. II. Spread awareness about preventive measures among villagers.
- Only I follows
- Only II follows
- [x] Both follow
- Neither follows
> Both are reasonable and complementary.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: Problem: "Some employees were found leaking confidential data." Actions: I. Take disciplinary action against those found guilty. II. Ban the use of computers in the office.
- Both follow
- [x] Only I follows
- Only II follows
- Neither follows
> II is drastic and would cripple the business.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Problem: "The price of onions has doubled because of a poor harvest." Actions: I. The government should release onions from its buffer stock. II. The government should ban the consumption of onions.
- Both follow
- [x] Only I follows
- Only II follows
- Neither follows
> Releasing stock increases supply, while a ban is unreasonable.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Problem: "Several cases of food poisoning were reported after a wedding." Action: The health department should test food samples from the caterer.
- [x] Follows
- Does not follow
- Follows only if someone dies
- Cannot be determined
> It is a direct, appropriate investigation.
:::

=== r-assertion-reason | Assertion and Reason
summary: Checking whether the assertion and the reason are each true, and whether the reason correctly explains the assertion.
---
Each question has an **Assertion (A)** and a **Reason (R)**. The standard options are:

1. Both A and R are true, and R **is** the correct explanation of A.
2. Both A and R are true, but R is **not** the correct explanation of A.
3. A is true, but R is false.
4. A is false, but R is true.
5. Both are false (sometimes offered).

## Three steps

1. Is A true on its own?
2. Is R true on its own?
3. If both are true, ask: **"A, because R"**. Does that sentence make sense as the explanation?

**Example.** A: "Iron rusts in moist air." R: "Iron reacts with oxygen and water to form hydrated iron oxide." Both are true, and R explains A: **option 1**.

:::mcq easy
Q: A: Water boils at a lower temperature on a mountain top. R: Air pressure is lower at high altitudes.
- [x] Both true, and R explains A
- Both true, but R doesn't explain A
- A true, R false
- A false, R true
> Lower pressure lowers the boiling point.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: A: The Sun rises in the east. R: The Earth rotates from west to east.
- [x] Both true, and R explains A
- Both true, but R doesn't explain A
- A true, R false
- A false, R true
> West-to-east rotation makes the Sun appear in the east.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A: Diamonds are very hard. R: Diamonds are expensive.
- Both true, and R explains A
- [x] Both true, but R doesn't explain A
- A true, R false
- A false, R true
> Price doesn't explain hardness; the crystal bonding does.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A: Bats are mammals. R: Bats can fly.
- Both true, and R explains A
- [x] Both true, but R doesn't explain A
- A true, R false
- A false, R true
> Bats are mammals because they nurse their young and have fur, not because they fly.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A: The Moon has no atmosphere. R: The Moon is larger than the Earth.
- Both true, and R explains A
- Both true, but R doesn't explain A
- [x] A true, R false
- A false, R true
> The Moon is much smaller than the Earth.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A: Plants release carbon dioxide during photosynthesis. R: Photosynthesis uses sunlight.
- Both true, and R explains A
- Both true, but R doesn't explain A
- A true, R false
- [x] A false, R true
> Photosynthesis releases oxygen, not carbon dioxide.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A: Ice floats on water. R: Ice is less dense than liquid water.
- [x] Both true, and R explains A
- Both true, but R doesn't explain A
- A true, R false
- A false, R true
> Lower density is exactly why it floats.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: A: Metals are good conductors of electricity. R: Metals have free electrons.
- [x] Both true, and R explains A
- Both true, but R doesn't explain A
- A true, R false
- A false, R true
> Free electrons carry the current.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: A: We see lightning before we hear thunder. R: Thunder is produced after lightning ends.
- Both true, and R explains A
- Both true, but R doesn't explain A
- [x] A true, R false
- A false, R true
> They happen together; light simply travels much faster than sound.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A: Salt is added to roads in winter in cold countries. R: Salt lowers the freezing point of water.
- [x] Both true, and R explains A
- Both true, but R doesn't explain A
- A true, R false
- A false, R true
> Salt stops ice forming on the roads.
:::
