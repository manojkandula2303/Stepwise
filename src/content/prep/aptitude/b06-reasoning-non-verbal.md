=== r-non-verbal | Non-Verbal Reasoning
summary: Pattern logic with shapes, arrows and grids: rotation, counting, cycling and matrices.
---
Non-verbal questions test the same pattern skills as series, but with **shapes** instead of numbers. Here they are written as symbols, so read each one carefully.

## What can change from one figure to the next

| Change | Look for |
|---|---|
| Rotation | an arrow or shape turns 45°, 90° or 180° each step |
| Count | the number of elements, sides or dots grows (often +1, +2, or triangular numbers) |
| Position | an element moves one cell clockwise, or along a row |
| Shading | fills in one part at a time, or alternates |
| Cycle | elements take turns in a fixed order |

## Matrix (3 × 3) problems

Usually **each shape appears once in every row and every column**, like a Sudoku. Find the shape that is missing from both the row and the column.

:::tip
Track one attribute at a time: first the position, then the count, then the shading. Several changes may happen at once.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: ↑, →, ↓, ←, ?
- →
- [x] ↑
- ↓
- ←
> The arrow turns 90° clockwise each step.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Triangle, square, pentagon, hexagon, ?
- Octagon
- [x] Heptagon
- Circle
- Triangle
> Each shape has one more side than the last (3, 4, 5, 6, then 7).
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: ○, ○○, ○○○, ?
- ○○
- [x] ○○○○
- ●●●●
- ○○○○○
> One more circle each time.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: ★☆☆, ☆★☆, ☆☆★, ?
- ☆☆☆
- [x] ★☆☆
- ★★☆
- ☆★★
> The filled star moves right one place and wraps around to the start.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: ↑ : ↓ :: → : ?
- ↑
- [x] ←
- →
- ↗
> The arrow is reversed (turned 180°).
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Find the odd one out: ↗, ↘, ↙, ↑
- ↗
- ↘
- ↙
- [x] ↑
> The others point diagonally.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Figures have 1, 3, 6 and 10 dots. How many dots does the next figure have?
- 13
- [x] 15
- 14
- 16
> Triangular numbers: the increase grows by 1 each time (+2, +3, +4, +5).
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: In a 3 × 3 grid, row 1 is ○ △ □ and row 2 is △ □ ○. Row 3 is □ ○ ?. Which shape is missing?
- ○
- [x] △
- □
- ☆
> Each shape appears once in every row and column; △ is missing from row 3 and column 3.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: ↑, ↗, →, ?
- ↓
- [x] ↘
- ←
- ↙
> The arrow turns 45° clockwise each step.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: A 2 × 2 grid is written as top row / bottom row. The grids go ■□/□□, then □■/□□, then □□/□■, then □□/■□. What comes next?
- □■/□□
- [x] ■□/□□
- □□/□■
- ■■/□□
> The black square moves clockwise around the grid: top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left, then back to top-left.
:::

=== r-mirror-images | Mirror Images
summary: Reflections in a vertical mirror: letters, words, arrows and clock times.
---
A **vertical mirror** (standing to the side) swaps **left and right**. Top and bottom stay where they are.

## Letters and words

- **Symmetric letters** look the same in a vertical mirror: **A H I M O T U V W X Y**.
- Other letters appear reversed (b ↔ d, p ↔ q).
- A word's letters appear in **reverse order**, and each letter is flipped. "TOY" reads **YOT**, because T, O and Y are all symmetric.

## Arrows

→ becomes ←, ↗ becomes ↖, and ↑ is unchanged.

## Clock times

**Mirror time = 11:60 − actual time** (for times between 1:00 and 11:00).

- 2:15 → **9:45**
- 10:10 → **1:50**
- For times from 12:00 to 12:59, use 23:60 − time: 12:35 → **11:25**.

The same formula works in reverse: if the mirror shows 4:20, the actual time is 11:60 − 4:20 = **7:40**.

:::mcq easy
Q: What does "b" look like in a vertical mirror?
- p
- [x] d
- q
- b
> Left and right swap, so b looks like d.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Which word looks the same in a vertical mirror?
- DAD
- [x] MOM
- BOB
- NUN
> MOM is a palindrome made only of symmetric letters. D, B and N flip.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: A clock shows 2:15. What does its mirror image show?
- 10:45
- [x] 9:45
- 9:15
- 3:45
> 11:60 − 2:15 = 9:45.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: A clock shows 10:10. What does its mirror image show?
- 2:50
- [x] 1:50
- 1:10
- 10:50
> 11:60 − 10:10 = 1:50.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: The mirror image of a clock shows 4:20. What is the actual time?
- 8:40
- [x] 7:40
- 7:20
- 4:40
> 11:60 − 4:20 = 7:40.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: How many letters of SYMMETRY look the same in a vertical mirror?
- 4
- [x] 5
- 6
- 3
> Y, M, M, T and Y are symmetric; S, E and R are not.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: What does "p" look like in a vertical mirror?
- d
- [x] q
- b
- p
> p flips left to right into q.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: What does → look like in a vertical mirror?
- ↑
- [x] ←
- →
- ↓
> Left and right swap.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: How does the word TOY read in a vertical mirror?
- TOY
- [x] YOT
- ⅄OT
- YOL
> The letters reverse order, and T, O and Y look unchanged.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: A clock shows 12:35. What does its mirror image show?
- 12:25
- [x] 11:25
- 11:35
- 1:25
> For times from 12:00 to 12:59, use 23:60 − 12:35 = 11:25.
:::

=== r-water-images | Water Images
summary: Reflections in a horizontal surface: letters, words, arrows and digits flipped top to bottom.
---
A **water image** is a reflection in a horizontal mirror (like a lake below an object). It swaps **top and bottom**; left and right stay where they are.

## Letters

- **Unchanged in water:** B C D E H I K O X (they are symmetric about a horizontal line).
- Common swaps: b ↔ p, d ↔ q, u ↔ n, M ↔ W.
- A word's letters stay **in the same order**; each one is flipped upside down.

## Arrows and digits

↑ becomes ↓, while → stays →. Digits 0, 3 and 8 look the same (1 nearly so).

| Mirror type | Swaps | Letter order |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical mirror | left ↔ right | reversed |
| Water image | top ↔ bottom | unchanged |

:::mcq easy
Q: What does "b" look like as a water image?
- d
- [x] p
- q
- b
> Top and bottom swap, so b becomes p.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: What does "d" look like as a water image?
- b
- [x] q
- p
- d
> d flips top to bottom into q.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Which word looks exactly the same as a water image?
- CAT
- [x] BOX
- MAP
- TOY
> B, O and X are all symmetric about a horizontal line, and the letter order doesn't change.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: What does ↑ look like as a water image?
- ↑
- [x] ↓
- →
- ←
> Top and bottom swap.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: What does → look like as a water image?
- ←
- [x] →
- ↑
- ↓
> A horizontal arrow is unchanged by a top-to-bottom flip.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: What does "M" look like as a water image?
- N
- [x] W
- M
- V
> Turned upside down, M looks like W.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Which digit looks the same as a water image?
- 7
- [x] 3
- 4
- 2
> 3 is symmetric about a horizontal line.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: In a water image of a word, what happens to the order of the letters?
- It is reversed
- [x] It stays the same
- It is shuffled
- Only vowels move
> Only top and bottom swap, so the left-to-right order is kept.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: What does "u" look like as a water image?
- v
- [x] n
- u
- m
> Upside down, u looks like n.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: How many letters of EXCHEQUER look exactly the same as a water image?
- 5
- [x] 6
- 7
- 4
> E, X, C, H, E and E are symmetric about a horizontal line; Q, U and R are not.
:::

=== r-paper-folding | Paper Folding and Cutting
summary: Predicting holes and cuts after folding a sheet one or more times and unfolding it.
---
## The doubling rule

Each fold doubles the number of layers. A single punch through all layers makes **2ⁿ holes** after n folds, as long as the punch is not on a fold line.

| Folds | Layers | Holes from one punch |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 2 | 4 | 4 |
| 3 | 8 | 8 |
| 4 | 16 | 16 |

## Symmetry

When you unfold, holes appear as **mirror images across each fold line**. Undo the folds in reverse order, reflecting the holes each time.

## Cuts on a fold

- A **semicircle cut on a folded edge** opens into a full circle (one hole, not two).
- A notch cut at the corner where **all the folds meet** (the centre of the sheet) opens into a single hole in the middle.
- A cut at the corner where all the **free edges** meet removes all four corners of the original sheet.

:::mcq easy
Q: A square sheet is folded in half once, and one hole is punched through both layers. How many holes are there when it is unfolded?
- 1
- [x] 2
- 4
- 3
> Two layers means two holes.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: A sheet is folded in half twice (into quarters), and one hole is punched through all the layers. How many holes are there when it is unfolded?
- 2
- [x] 4
- 8
- 6
> 2² = 4 layers.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: A sheet is folded in half three times, and one hole is punched. How many holes are there when it is unfolded?
- 6
- [x] 8
- 9
- 16
> 2³ = 8 layers.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: A sheet is folded once vertically and a hole is punched. How are the holes positioned when it is unfolded?
- Both on the left half
- [x] Mirror images of each other across the fold line
- Both at the top
- In random positions
> Unfolding reflects each hole across the fold.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A square sheet is folded once along a diagonal, and a hole is punched near the middle of one half. What do you see when it is unfolded?
- One hole
- [x] Two holes, symmetric about the diagonal
- Four holes
- Two holes, symmetric about the vertical centre line
> The diagonal is the fold line, so the two holes mirror each other across it.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A sheet is folded in half and a semicircle is cut from the folded edge. What do you see when it is unfolded?
- Two semicircles
- [x] One full circle
- Two circles
- A semicircle at the edge
> The cut opens up across the fold into a single circle.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A square sheet is folded into quarters, and a small notch is cut at the corner where both folds meet. How many holes are there when it is unfolded?
- 4
- [x] 1
- 2
- 0
> That corner is the centre of the sheet, so the notch opens into one hole in the middle.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A strip of paper is folded in half four times, and one hole is punched. How many holes are there when it is unfolded?
- 8
- [x] 16
- 32
- 4
> 2⁴ = 16 layers.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A square sheet is folded into quarters, and the corner where all the free edges meet is cut off. How many corners of the original square are cut when it is unfolded?
- 1
- 2
- [x] 4
- 0
> The four original corners are stacked at that point, so all four are cut.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: A square is folded in half from top to bottom, then in half from left to right. A semicircle is cut along the second (vertical) folded edge, away from the corners. How many holes are there when it is unfolded?
- 1
- [x] 2
- 4
- 3
> Unfolding the vertical fold turns the semicircle into a full circle. That circle is in both layers of the first fold, so there are 2 circles, mirrored top and bottom.
:::

=== r-figure-series | Figure Series
summary: Predicting the next figure when several elements move, rotate, grow or change shading.
---
A figure series is a sequence of figures that change by fixed rules. Here the figures are written as symbol strings.

## Rules to test, in order

1. **Movement:** an element moves one position at a time (and may wrap around).
2. **Rotation:** an arrow or shape turns by a fixed angle.
3. **Count:** elements are added or removed.
4. **Shading:** one more part is shaded each step, or shading alternates.
5. **Several rules at once:** for example, an arrow rotates while a dot count increases. Check each element separately.

**Example.** "A _ _ B", "_ A B _", "_ B A _", ? Here A moves right one place each step and B moves left one place, so next comes **"B _ _ A"**.

:::mcq easy
Q: ▲, ▲▲, ▲▲▲, ?
- ▲▲
- [x] ▲▲▲▲
- ▼▼▼▼
- ▲▲▲▲▲
> One more triangle each step.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: ●○●○, ○●○●, ●○●○, ?
- ●○●○
- [x] ○●○●
- ●●○○
- ○○●●
> The two patterns alternate.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: "X _ _ _", "_ X _ _", "_ _ X _", ?
- "X _ _ _"
- [x] "_ _ _ X"
- "_ X _ _"
- "_ _ X X"
> X moves one place to the right each step.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "A _ _ B", "_ A B _", "_ B A _", ?
- "A _ _ B"
- [x] "B _ _ A"
- "_ A B _"
- "B A _ _"
> A moves right and B moves left, one place each step.

:::mcq easy
Q: □□□□, ■□□□, ■■□□, ?
- ■■■■
- [x] ■■■□
- □■■■
- ■□■□

:::

:::mcq easy
Q: ←, ↑, →, ?
- ←
- [x] ↓
- ↑

> The arrow turns 90° clockwise each step.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Hexagon, pentagon, square, ?
- Circle
- [x] Triangle
- Hexagon
- Rectangle
> One fewer side each step (6, 5, 4, then 3).
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: 1 black dot, 3 white dots, 5 black dots, ?
- 6 white dots
- [x] 7 white dots
- 7 black dots
- 9 white dots
> The count goes up by 2 and the colour alternates.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: AB, BC, CD, ?

- [x] DE
- EF

> Each letter moves forward by 1.
:::

:::mcq hard

=== r-embedded-figures | Embedded Figures and Counting
summary: Counting triangles, squares and rectangles in figures, and finding a pattern hidden inside a larger one.
---
## Counting formulas

| Figure | Count |
|---|---|
| Squares in an n × n grid | 1² + 2² + … + n² |
| Rectangles in an m × n grid | ᵐ⁺¹C₂ × ⁿ⁺¹C₂ |
| Triangles in a triangle with k lines from the apex to the base | (k + 1)(k + 2)/2 |
| Square with both diagonals | 8 triangles |

- 2 × 2 grid: 4 + 1 = **5 squares**; ³C₂ × ³C₂ = **9 rectangles**
- 3 × 3 grid: 9 + 4 + 1 = **14 squares**

## Systematic counting

Count by **size**: first the single smallest pieces, then shapes made of 2 pieces, then 3, and so on. Or count by **vertex**: list every triangle that has a particular corner. Never count by eye alone.

## Embedded patterns

To check whether a small pattern hides inside a larger one, slide it along the larger one one position at a time, the same way you'd search for a word.

:::mcq easy
Q: A triangle has one line drawn from its apex to its base. How many triangles are there?
- 2
- [x] 3
- 4
- 1
> The two small triangles plus the whole one.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A square has both diagonals drawn. How many triangles are there?
- 4
- [x] 8
- 6
- 10
> 4 small triangles plus 4 made of two small ones (each half of the square).
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: How many squares are in a 2 × 2 grid?
- 4
- [x] 5
- 6
- 8
> 4 small squares plus 1 large one.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: How many squares are in a 3 × 3 grid?
- 9
- [x] 14
- 13
- 12
> 9 + 4 + 1 = 14.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: How many rectangles (squares included) are in a 2 × 2 grid?
- 5
- [x] 9
- 8
- 6
> ³C₂ × ³C₂ = 3 × 3 = 9.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Which of these patterns appears inside □■□■■□■?
- ■■■
- □□■
- [x] □■■
- ■□□
> Positions 3 to 5 read □■■.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Which word is hidden, in order, in CARPETRAIN?
- RAT
- [x] PET
- PEAR
- TIN
> C-A-R-**P-E-T**-R-A-I-N.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A triangle has two lines drawn from its apex to its base. How many triangles are there?
- 4
- [x] 6
- 5
- 3
> (2 + 1)(2 + 2)/2 = 6.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: What is the fewest straight lines needed to draw a square with both diagonals?
- 4
- [x] 6
- 5
- 8
> 4 sides + 2 diagonals.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: Triangle ABC has a line from A to the base BC, and a line parallel to BC crossing the other two sides and that first line. How many triangles are there?
- 4
- [x] 6
- 8
- 5
> Above the parallel line there are 3 (two small ones and their union). Using the whole height there are 3 more (left, right and the whole triangle).
:::

=== r-cubes-dice | Cubes and Dice
summary: Painted-cube counting, opposite faces of dice, and deducing faces from several views.
---
## Painted cubes

A cube painted on every face and cut into n × n × n small cubes gives:

| Painted faces | Count | Where they are |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 8 | corners |
| 2 | 12(n − 2) | edges |
| 1 | 6(n − 2)² | face centres |
| 0 | (n − 2)³ | inside |

For n = 3: 8, 12, 6, 1 (total 27). For n = 4: 8, 24, 24, 8 (total 64).

For a cuboid a × b × c, cubes with exactly 2 painted faces = 4[(a − 2) + (b − 2) + (c − 2)].

## Dice

- On a standard die, **opposite faces add up to 7**: 1–6, 2–5, 3–4.
- Faces that appear **together** in any view are **adjacent**, so they cannot be opposite.
- If a face is adjacent to four different faces across several views, it is opposite the one remaining face.
- If two views share one face **in the same position**, the faces in matching positions in the two views are opposite each other.

A cube has 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices.

:::mcq easy
Q: A painted cube is cut into 27 equal small cubes. How many small cubes have 3 painted faces?
- 6
- [x] 8
- 12
- 4
> The 8 corners.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: A painted cube is cut into 27 equal small cubes. How many have exactly 2 painted faces?
- 8
- [x] 12
- 6
- 24
> 12(3 − 2) = 12, one on each edge.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: A painted cube is cut into 27 equal small cubes. How many have exactly 1 painted face?
- 8
- [x] 6
- 12
- 1
> 6(3 − 2)² = 6, one in the centre of each face.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: A painted cube is cut into 27 equal small cubes. How many have no painted face?
- 0
- [x] 1
- 8
- 6
> (3 − 2)³ = 1, the cube in the very centre.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: A painted cube is cut into 64 equal small cubes. How many have no painted face?
- 1
- [x] 8
- 16
- 27
> (4 − 2)³ = 8.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: On a standard die, which face is opposite 3?
- 5
- [x] 4
- 6
- 2
> Opposite faces add up to 7.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Two views of a die both show 1 on top. In the first, 2 is at the front and 3 on the right. In the second, 4 is at the front and 5 on the right. Which face is opposite 2?
- 3
- [x] 4
- 5
- 6
> The common face (1) is in the same position, so the faces in matching positions are opposite: 2–4 and 3–5.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Across several views of a die, the faces seen next to 1 are 2, 3, 4 and 6. Which face is opposite 1?
- 6
- [x] 5
- 2
- Cannot be determined
> A face has exactly 4 neighbours, so the remaining face, 5, must be opposite.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: How many edges does a cube have?
- 8
- [x] 12
- 6
- 24
> 4 on the top, 4 on the bottom and 4 vertical.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: A 4 × 3 × 2 cuboid is painted on every face and cut into 1 × 1 × 1 cubes. How many cubes have exactly 2 painted faces?
- 8
- [x] 12
- 16
- 4
> 4[(4 − 2) + (3 − 2) + (2 − 2)] = 4 × 3 = 12.
:::
