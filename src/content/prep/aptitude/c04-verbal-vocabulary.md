=== v-vocabulary | Vocabulary Building
summary: Roots, prefixes and suffixes that unlock unfamiliar words, plus high-frequency exam words.
---
Memorising word lists is slow. **Roots and affixes** let you work out words you have never seen.

## High-value roots

| Root | Meaning | Words |
|---|---|---|
| bene | good | benefit, benevolent |
| mal | bad | malicious, malfunction |
| chron | time | chronic, chronology |
| graph | write | autograph, graphic |
| loqu / log | speak | eloquent, dialogue |
| ver | true | verify, veracity |
| omni | all | omnipresent, omnivore |
| ambi | both | ambiguous, ambivalent |

## Prefixes and suffixes

- **un-, in-, im-, ir-, dis-** mean *not*: irregular, dishonest, impartial.
- **-cide** means killing (pesticide); **-phobia** means fear (claustrophobia); **-ous / -ful** mean full of.

## High-frequency exam words

**Ubiquitous** (found everywhere) · **meticulous** (very careful) · **pragmatic** (practical) · **candid** (frank) · **ephemeral** (short-lived) · **diligent** (hard-working) · **lucid** (clear) · **obsolete** (out of date) · **resilient** (bounces back) · **verbose** (wordy)

:::tip
Learn words in **pairs of opposites** (verbose ↔ concise, ephemeral ↔ permanent). One effort gives you both a synonym and an antonym.
:::

=== v-synonyms | Synonyms
summary: Finding the word closest in meaning, while watching for traps of tone and degree.
---
A **synonym** has the closest meaning to the given word. The options often include:

- an **antonym**, to catch a hasty reader
- a word that is **related but different** in meaning
- a word that is **similar but weaker or stronger**

## Method

1. Put the word in a sentence you know.
2. Swap in each option and keep the one that preserves the meaning **and** the tone.
3. If you're unsure, use roots: *loqu* = speak, so "loquacious" means talkative.

## Useful pairs

abundant = plentiful · candid = frank · diligent = industrious · eminent = distinguished · frugal = thrifty · lucid = clear · obstinate = stubborn · placid = calm · reticent = reserved · vivid = bright

=== v-antonyms | Antonyms
summary: Finding the word most opposite in meaning, and avoiding near-opposites and synonym traps.
---
An **antonym** is the word most nearly **opposite** in meaning. The traps:

- a **synonym** placed among the options
- a word that is merely **different**, not opposite
- prefix look-alikes (*in-* doesn't always mean "not": *inflammable* means flammable)

## Method

1. Define the word clearly.
2. Think of its opposite before reading the options.
3. Check the option that matches your opposite in **degree and tone**.

## Useful pairs

ancient ↔ modern · artificial ↔ natural · expand ↔ contract · humble ↔ arrogant · transparent ↔ opaque · verbose ↔ concise · permanent ↔ temporary · scarce ↔ abundant · praise ↔ criticise · optimist ↔ pessimist

=== v-one-word-substitution | One-Word Substitution
summary: Replacing a phrase with a single precise word: people, fears, killings, government types and more.
---
These test whether you know the single word that means a whole phrase. Learn them by **category**.

| Category | Phrase → word |
|---|---|
| People | one who knows many languages → **polyglot**; one who doesn't believe in God → **atheist**; one who hates mankind → **misanthrope** |
| Fear (-phobia) | fear of heights → **acrophobia**; fear of closed spaces → **claustrophobia** |
| Killing (-cide) | killing of a king → **regicide**; of one's brother → **fratricide** |
| Government (-cracy) | rule by a few → **oligarchy**; by the wealthy → **plutocracy** |
| Places | a place where birds are kept → **aviary**; where bees are kept → **apiary** |
| Writing | one's own life story → **autobiography**; a speech made without preparation → **extempore** |
| Other | something that cannot be read → **illegible**; lasting a very short time → **ephemeral** |

=== v-word-usage | Word Usage
summary: Choosing the sentence where a word is used correctly, especially with confusable pairs.
---
Word-usage questions test **confusable words** and **words with several meanings**.

## Confusable pairs

| Pair | Use |
|---|---|
| affect / effect | affect is the verb; effect is the noun |
| accept / except | receive / excluding |
| advice / advise | noun / verb |
| complement / compliment | complete / praise |
| principal / principle | head or main / a rule or belief |
| stationary / stationery | not moving / paper goods |
| lose / loose | misplace / not tight |
| its / it's | belonging to it / it is |
| council / counsel | a governing group / advice |

## Words with several meanings

One word can be different parts of speech with different senses, for example *bank* (a river edge, a financial institution, to rely on). In "which sentence uses X incorrectly" questions, check the grammar of each sentence too.

:::mcq easy
Q: Which sentence uses "affect" correctly?
- The affect of the drug was quick.
- [x] Lack of sleep can affect your memory.
- The movie had a great affect on me.
- Affects are visible.
> "Affect" is a verb.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Which sentence is correct?
- Can you give me some advise?
- [x] Can you advise me on this?
- She advised me an advise.
- His advise was good.
> "Advise" is the verb; "advice" is the noun.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Which sentence is correct?
- The car was stationery at the signal.
- [x] Buy some stationery for the office.
- The stationery train left.
- He stood stationery.
> Stationery means paper goods; stationary means not moving.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Which sentence is correct?
- Its raining outside.
- [x] The dog wagged its tail.
- It's tail is long.
- Its' a good day.
> "Its" is possessive; "it's" means "it is".
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Which sentence is correct?
- The principle of the school spoke.
- [x] Honesty is a principle I live by.
- She is the principle dancer.
- The principle amount is due.
> A principle is a rule or belief.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Which sentence is correct?
- She complemented me on my dress.
- [x] The wine complements the meal perfectly.
- He gave a nice complement.
- I complement you on your work.
> To complement is to complete or enhance; to compliment is to praise.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Which sentence is correct?
- Don't loose your keys.
- [x] These trousers are too loose.
- I always loose games.
- Loose weight fast.
> Loose means not tight; lose means misplace.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Which sentence is correct?
- Everyone came accept Ravi.
- [x] Please accept my apology.
- I except your invitation.
- She excepted the award.
> Accept means receive; except means excluding.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Which sentence is correct?
- The city counsel met today.
- [x] He sought legal counsel before signing.
- She is a member of the counsel.
- The counsel of ministers decided.
> Counsel means advice; a council is a governing group.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: In which sentence is "bank" used as a verb?
- He sat on the river bank.
- She works at a bank.
- [x] You can bank on me to help.
- The bank was closed.
> "Bank on" means rely on.
:::

=== v-contextual-vocabulary | Contextual Vocabulary
summary: Working out the meaning of a word from the sentence around it.
---
The question asks what a word means **in this sentence**. Often it's a common word used in a less common sense.

## Method

1. Cover the word and predict a replacement from the context.
2. Find the option that matches **your prediction**, not the word's most familiar meaning.
3. Use **contrast and example signals**: "unlike", "but", "such as", "for instance".

**Example.** "The negotiations **foundered** after the two sides refused to compromise." Foundered = failed (originally, a ship sinking).

:::mcq medium
Q: "The negotiations foundered when neither side would compromise." Here, "foundered" means:
- Began
- [x] Failed
- Succeeded
- Continued
> Refusing to compromise led to collapse.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "His explanation was so opaque that nobody understood it." Here, "opaque" means:
- Transparent
- [x] Hard to understand
- Short
- Funny
> The result (nobody understood) defines the word.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "The manager tried to placate the angry customer with a refund." Here, "placate" means:
- Provoke
- [x] Calm down
- Ignore
- Blame
> A refund is meant to soothe anger.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: "Unlike his gregarious brother, Arun prefers to be alone." Here, "gregarious" means:
- Shy
- [x] Sociable
- Greedy
- Serious
> "Unlike" contrasts it with preferring to be alone.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "The report was a cursory glance at the problem, missing important details." Here, "cursory" means:
- Thorough
- [x] Hasty and superficial
- Angry
- Expert
> Missing details implies a quick, shallow look.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "Despite the setbacks, the team remained sanguine about winning." Here, "sanguine" means:
- Bloody
- [x] Optimistic
- Fearful
- Angry
> "Despite the setbacks" points to a positive outlook.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "The old law was finally repealed after years of criticism." Here, "repealed" means:
- Strengthened
- [x] Cancelled
- Repeated
- Explained
> Criticism led to it being withdrawn.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: "Her laconic reply, a single 'No', ended the discussion." Here, "laconic" means:
- Lengthy
- [x] Using very few words
- Rude
- Confused
> A one-word reply illustrates it.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: "The company will capitalise on the festival season to boost sales." Here, "capitalise on" means:
- Write in capitals
- [x] Take advantage of
- Invest money in
- Avoid
> It means using the opportunity.
:::

:::mcq hard
Q: "The minister's speech was full of platitudes but offered no real plan." Here, "platitudes" means:
- Detailed policies
- [x] Dull, overused remarks
- Jokes
- Statistics
> They are contrasted with a real plan: empty, clichéd statements.
:::

=== v-verbal-analogies | Verbal Analogies
summary: Word-pair relationships such as synonym, antonym, degree, part–whole, tool–user and cause–effect.
---
**A : B :: C : D.** Find the precise relationship between A and B, then choose the pair (or word) with the **same** relationship.

## Relationship types

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Synonym | Happy : Joyful |
| Antonym | Rich : Poor |
| Degree | Warm : Hot, Like : Love |
| Part : whole | Page : Book |
| Tool : user | Scalpel : Surgeon |
| Worker : product | Poet : Poem |
| Cause : effect | Virus : Disease |
| Category : member | Fruit : Apple |
| Young : adult | Lamb : Sheep |

:::tip
Write the relationship as a sentence ("A scalpel is a tool used by a surgeon") and test each option with **the same sentence**.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Happy : Joyful :: Sad : ?
- Angry
- [x] Sorrowful
- Cheerful
- Tired
> Synonyms.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Rich : Poor :: Strong : ?
- Powerful
- [x] Weak
- Tall
- Brave
> Antonyms.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Warm : Hot :: Cool : ?
- Warm
- [x] Cold
- Mild
- Wet
> Degree: hot is more intense than warm, and cold is more intense than cool.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Page : Book :: Brick : ?

- [x] Wall
- Clay
- House-builder
> Part : whole.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Scalpel : Surgeon :: Brush : ?
- Canvas
- [x] Painter
- Paint
- Colour
> Tool : user.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Poet : Poem :: Architect : ?

- [x] Design
- Brick
- Engineer
> Worker : product.
:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Virus : Disease :: Fire : ?

- [x] Smoke
- Wood
- Heat-proof
> Cause : effect.
:::

:::mcq easy
Q: Lamb : Sheep :: Kid : ?

- [x] Goat
- Cow

:::

:::mcq medium
Q: Like : Love :: Dislike : ?

- Admire

:::

:::mcq hard
Q: Pen : Write :: Scissors : ?
- Paper
- [x] Cut
- Sharp
- Tailor
> Tool : function.
:::
